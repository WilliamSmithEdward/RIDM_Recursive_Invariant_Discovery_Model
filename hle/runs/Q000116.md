## RIDM 22 Application

A two-part identification item: name the arthropod in a photo and judge
one disease-transmission risk. The photo was instrumented (crop,
upscale, contrast) rather than eyeballed; the discriminating observable
was ornate pale mottling across the dorsum, which types the genus and
excludes the vector genus for the asked disease. The context givens
(equipment brand, lawn, questing posture on gear) supported the
regional and behavioral fit. The risk verdict was then checked for
robustness across the plausible identification set: every credible
rival identification yields the same negative verdict, and only the
confidently excluded genus would flip it, so part (b) carried more
support than part (a) alone would give it.

## Framework Observations

- Robustness partitioning did real work: when a compound answer's
  second slot is invariant across the first slot's plausible
  candidates, the compound band is set by the first slot only, and the
  verdict slot is safe even under residual ID uncertainty. This is the
  converse of the weakest-slot rule: check whether the slots are
  actually coupled before inheriting the weakest link.
- The item is the classic teaching pair (a feared disease paired with a
  non-vector species); recognizing the pedagogical construction
  supported the identification rather than replacing it.

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: instrument photo identifications,
then test whether downstream verdict slots are invariant across the
credible identification set; an invariant verdict decouples from the
identification band.
