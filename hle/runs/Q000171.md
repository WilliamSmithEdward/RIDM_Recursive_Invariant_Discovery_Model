## RIDM 31 Application

A single-sentence item asking for the smallest field-multiplication count of one small
linear combination of two affine points on a curve family, with the result in a named
extended coordinate system and the cost conventions of a public formula catalogue restated
in the stem. Typed casually authored: informal grammar, the catalogue's cost idiom, and a
stated simplification of the catalogue's cost classes. The rigorous route ran first.
Negation is free in this family, so the combination was rewritten as a doubling of a
difference followed by one mixed addition, and each step was costed from the catalogue's
formulas with the two known savings: skipping the auxiliary coordinate when the next
operation is a doubling, and forming the curve-constant term from coordinate products when
both inputs are affine. That total came out six below the cheapest route that computes the
two scalar multiples separately. The separate route was enumerated, including a
tripling-based variant, and costed above the committed route on the recalled assumption
that the catalogue's tripling formula outputs a coordinate system needing a conversion.
Referent variants were priced at one and two multiplications either side for accounting
conventions, and the value class was fixed as a small positive integer.

The key is the separate route: a doubling formula specialized to an affine input, a
tripling formula from the same catalogue that likewise never reads the auxiliary
coordinate, and one general addition, summed from the catalogue's published counts. The
rationale's whole insight is that neither formula needs the auxiliary coordinate, so no
conversion is paid.

## Framework Observations

- Candidate coverage failed at the recall layer. The author's route was generated, but it
  was costed on an absence claim, that the catalogue holds no tripling formula in the
  target coordinate system, which was recall typed as reported and never probed. The
  catalogue does hold one, and with it the author's route lands on the keyed value. An
  absence claim about a public catalogue is exactly the recall the framework says stays
  assumed until probed, and it was used to demote a route.
- The referent was mis-modeled. On a casually authored extremal item the key is the
  author's optimum over the author's own search space, and the search space is
  fingerprinted by the stem's idiom: a stem written in a catalogue's cost notation is
  answered by composing catalogue entries in the shape of the stated expression, one entry
  per stated coefficient. Rewriting the expression is an algorithmic move the catalogue
  does not name, so it lies outside the space an author of this register searches. The
  committed route is the true minimum and is not the key.
- The band was right. Low was committed and the miss landed at the low band, but the fork
  that was priced, accounting conventions, was not the decisive one. The decisive link was
  the model of the author's search space, which never entered the ledger.
- Key-quality note: the keyed value is not the smallest cost. The committed route beats it
  by six under the key's own accounting and by at least four under any accounting the

## Lessons

- An extremal claim on a casually authored item, smallest or largest or best, is the
  author's optimum over a search space the stem's idiom fingerprints. The composition that
  mirrors the stated expression's shape is the primary referent candidate, and a global
  optimum that first rewrites the expression is a priced rival, not the referent.
- A recalled absence, that a catalogue, table, or library lacks an entry, is typed
  reported and cannot demote a route. Cost the route under the assumption that the entry
  exists at its plausible published value before ranking.
