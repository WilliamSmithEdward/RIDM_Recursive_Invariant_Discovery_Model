## RIDM 15 Application

A which-statements-must-be-true item over a fully enumerated idealized population model,
with a large combinatorial menu including a none option. The attempt derived three of the
four statement verdicts exactly as the key later reasoned them, including the two designed
traps: premises scoped to the present cannot bind future generations, and a heritability
below one plus no environment-uniformity premise permits a systematic regional phenotype
difference. The single decisive link was the fourth statement, which turns on what one
term of art in the stem entails. Two readings were constructed and named before
commitment: the textbook-definition reading, under which the term directly entails the
statement, and a maximal-strictness reading, under which real-world edge cases the
idealization abstracts away block the entailment. The attempt committed on maximal
strictness; the key used the textbook definition, stating it verbatim.

## Framework Observations

- Second consecutive run in which the keyed answer was the explicitly named rival,
  rejected by a meta-prior about the author rather than by evidence. Here the prior was
  that a must-be-true item rewards maximal skepticism uniformly; the author instead
  applied skepticism only to premise scope, never to term definitions.
- Two kinds of strictness were conflated. Scope strictness, refusing extrapolation
  beyond what the premises cover, drove the two trap statements correctly. Definitional
  skepticism, doubting that a term of art entails its curricular gloss, is not how
  authored items work: inside a stated idealized model, terms carry their textbook
  meanings and definitional entailments count as must-be-true.
- The counterexample that drove the wrong ranking imported outside-the-model ecology
  into a model-vocabulary term, a category error the framework's own invariant-scope
  rule (an invariant outside its scope is not a stronger answer) should have caught.
- Author-competence machinery misfired in the same direction as the previous run:
  the standard-curriculum reading beat the design-elegance reading, twice.

## Lessons

1. Distinguish entailment-by-definition from entailment-by-extrapolation. On authored
   must-be-true items, the author denies extrapolations beyond premise scope but
   affirms the definitional content of terms of art; skepticism aimed at a textbook
   definition is misplaced rigor.
2. Within an idealized-model stem, evaluate entailments inside the model: a term of art
   means its curricular definition, and edge cases from nature that the idealization
   abstracts away do not defeat the entailment.
3. When two live readings split as standard-curriculum versus design-philosophy, the
   curriculum reading leads on items authored in curricular domains; a meta-prior about
   item-design elegance is narrative-tier and cannot outrank it.
