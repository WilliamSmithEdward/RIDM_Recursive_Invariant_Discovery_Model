## RIDM 14 Application

A jurisdiction-and-date-pinned compliance question: five described distributions, one of
which fits a prospectus exemption. The attempt classified the item as well-posed (precise
entity names, dollar figures, an as-of date) and analyzed each option independently
against a small recalled rule set: a debt-securities exemption for scheduled banks with no
purchaser-type restriction, equity securities falling outside it, and the accredited
investor and private-issuer thresholds. The committed option was the counterintuitive one,
a foreign-sounding name whose national suffix marks it as the domestically incorporated
scheduled entity; the parallel foreign-parent option was rejected on exactly that
entity-precision ground. A repeated decoy detail (a destitute investor appearing in two
options) was assigned its direction of force: it tempts a wealth-based rule that does not
exist, and discriminates nothing since it appears in both. The reveal matched the chain
point for point, including the entity distinction and the debt-versus-equity boundary.

## Framework Observations

- This is what successful recall-backed commitment looks like, in contrast to the failed
  recall two runs earlier: the recalled material was stable statutory structure rather
  than one study's finding, and the item provided a global coherence check, since a single
  consistent rule set had to explain the status of all five options with no leftovers.
  Recall validated by whole-menu coherence is materially stronger than recall validated by
  availability.
- Lexical precision in entity names carried the discriminating content twice in one item
  (the national suffix on the compliant issuer; the parent-versus-subsidiary naming on the
  rejected one). The verbatim-form discipline from the previous runs generalizes: names in
  authored items are chosen exactly, and near-forms are distinct legal facts.
- The shared decoy pattern: a vivid detail duplicated across two options cannot
  discriminate between them and is therefore aimed at a rule the reader is tempted to
  invent. Assigning it that direction of force neutralized it cheaply.

## Lessons

1. Grade recall by its validation surface, not its vividness: recall that must jointly
   explain every option under one rule set (whole-menu coherence) earns a band that
   isolated single-fact recall does not.
2. A detail duplicated across options discriminates nothing; ask what rule it tempts the
   reader to invent, and check whether that rule exists before letting the detail move any
   ranking.
