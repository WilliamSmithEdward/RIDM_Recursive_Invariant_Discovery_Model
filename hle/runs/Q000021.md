## RIDM 13 Application

A transcription task: a single, heavily ligatured word in a late Byzantine hand, with the
diacritic required in the answer. The attempt upscaled, cropped, and contrast-enhanced the
image before interpreting it (cheap read-only observation repeated before commitment),
segmented the ink into candidate letter strokes, and treated recalled ligature conventions
as reported evidence to be checked against structure rather than trusted directly.

The decisive residual was one tall ligature stroke readable as either of two letters, plus a
long diagonal readable as either a letter or a diacritic written in one motion with its
vowel. Candidate coverage enumerated the joint readings of the whole string; all but one
produced either a non-word or a reading incoherent with the task's own guarantees (one word,
one accent, a form of a common lemma), and the coherence rule refuted them. The surviving
reading was committed at medium confidence. The reveal confirmed both decisive calls,
including the diacritic fused into the vowel stroke.

## Framework Observations

- Perception tasks fit the ledger cleanly: strokes are observed, letter identities are
  inferred, ligature conventions are reported. Ranking joint readings by what the observed
  ink plus the task's guarantees refute worked better than recognizing glyphs one at a time.
- The task's guarantee sentence acted as a discriminative feature under Section 23, not
  boilerplate: "a form of a common word" eliminated a rarer-lemma reading that fit the ink
  about equally well.
- Progressive re-observation (zoom, crop, contrast) is the image analogue of the
  reversibility ladder: repeat cheap read-only observation before committing.

## Lessons

1. For image transcription, run the coherence check over joint readings of the whole string,
   not per glyph; non-word outcomes refute otherwise plausible stroke readings.
2. A task's guarantees about its own answer (wordhood, commonness, diacritics present) are
   discriminative evidence for ranking readings, in the same sense as Section 23's
   emphasized qualifiers.
