## RIDM 17 Application

A satellite-image identification item: a faint square earthwork at stated
coordinates, with a two-part ask (identify the structure; is it a designated
landmark). The rigorous route was geographic: decode the coordinates, place them
relative to a major river's course at that longitude, and derive the country,
state, and district before any structure recall. That narrowing left exactly one
famous square ruin in the district, an eighteenth-century colonial frontier fort
from a documented fort line, and the terrain features in the image (a vegetated
arroyo west of the structure, a terrace siting) cohered with that site's recorded
setting. The designation sub-answer was inferred from the jurisdiction's blanket
statutory protection of colonial-era immovable structures rather than from recall
of a specific decree. Both components matched the key; the key's rationale noted
that a frontier-model answer confuses the site with a similarly named county
across the border, a trap the coordinate derivation never made available.

  point falls on, from the river's latitude at that longitude, did the decisive

  unique-selection probe (square colonial ruin in that specific district) rather
  than availability.
- The stem's own assertion that the structure is ancient was treated as a

- Banding: the compound answer's weakest link was the inferred designation

## Lessons

1. On coordinate-anchored identification items, derive the jurisdiction first:
   administrative side-of-boundary facts (which country, which protection regime)
