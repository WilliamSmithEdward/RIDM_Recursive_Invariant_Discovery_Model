## RIDM 13 Application

A raster-plot measurement task: identify synchronized population bursts and report the
average inter-burst interval to two significant figures. The attempt instrumented the image
as in the previous measurement run: axis ticks located programmatically and fit to a linear
time map, column-wise spike density computed, smoothed, and thresholded, and the burst count
verified robust by confirming the next-strongest density feature sits far below the four
detected events. The four burst locations matched the official ones to within fractions of
a second.

The miss was at the last step. The measured average landed within the measurement's own
uncertainty of a rounding boundary, making the discretized answer effectively bimodal, and
the attempt's peak-density timestamp convention picked one side while the official
onset-style timestamps pick the other. The residual was explicitly named and priced before
the reveal (the band was capped at medium for exactly this risk), but the tie-break was
treated as noise when it was actually resolvable: the domain's canonical burst timestamp is
the burst onset, and recomputing under the onset convention moves the average across the
boundary to the official side.

## Framework Observations

- Instrumented measurement again produced event locations accurate to fractions of a
  second; the pipeline (calibrate from all reference features, detect programmatically,
  verify absences) transfers across image tasks.
- A discretized answer near a rounding boundary is a compound claim: the measurement plus a
  convention. When the continuous value sits within uncertainty of the boundary, the
  convention becomes the decisive claim and must be resolved from domain practice, not
  defaulted from the pipeline's internal representation.
- Correctly pricing a residual into the band is not the same as resolving it. The
  medium-band call was vindicated as calibration, but the residual was resolvable at low
  cost, and resolving it would have converted a priced risk into a correct answer.

## Lessons

1. Event timestamps follow domain conventions (a burst is timestamped at onset, not peak
   density); adopt the domain convention before deriving any quantity that discretizes.
2. When a measured quantity lands within its uncertainty of a rounding or threshold
   boundary, treat the two discretized answers as live candidates and spend effort on the
   convention or definition that separates them, rather than only lowering confidence.
