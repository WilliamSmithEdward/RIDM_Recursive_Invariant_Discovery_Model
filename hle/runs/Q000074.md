## RIDM 18 Application

A cycle-budget question: a named production vehicle at a stated engine speed,
a stated processor clock, one interrupt per revolution, and a request for the
maximum cycles available to the algorithm that determines each spark firing.
The bare arithmetic gives the per-revolution window in one division. The
item's most deliberate construction was the vehicle specification: the model
year and trim combination uniquely pins the engine to a six-cylinder, with
the alternatives (the V8, the plug-in four) excluded by exactly that year and
trim. A reading under which the answer is the bare window leaves that
construction decorative, so the load-bearingness test demoted it.

The committed chain divided the window by the firing events per revolution:
a four-stroke six fires each plug once per two revolutions, so three events
per revolution, and the per-event budget is the window over three. An
independent route converged: consecutive events are a third of a revolution
apart, and that interval times the clock gives the same number. Rivals were
computed to parity: the whole-window reading (complete, but vehicle
unconsumed), the six-events reading (requires wasted spark or a four-stroke
error, against the automotive care shown in the year and trim pinning), and
a V8 misrecall (contradicted by the model-year pin). The key matched the
committed chain step for step, and its rationale states that the models it
was tested against all ignored cylinder count and strokes.
