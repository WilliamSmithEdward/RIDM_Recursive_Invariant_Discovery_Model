## RIDM 13 Application

A quantitative reading task: find the longest interval between two waveform peaks on a
scanned rhythm strip and convert it to seconds using the stated paper speed. Rather than
eyeballing the strip, the attempt measured it: grid pitch calibrated by averaging across two
hundred fifty gridlines, peak positions detected programmatically in every strip row by
vertical dark-run analysis, and the candidate pause swept for low-amplitude beats that a
visual scan might miss (none present, maximum deflection inside the pause was baseline
scale). The interval came out to exactly twenty-six large boxes, and the official rationale's
hand count in millimeters matches the programmatic measurement to the millimeter.

## Framework Observations

- For measurement tasks over images, converting perception into instrumented measurement
  upgrades the claim type from inferred to observed. The calibration step (averaging pitch
  over the full grid rather than a few boxes) is what made the exact-match precision safe.
- The hidden-beat sweep is the null-candidate discipline applied to signal reading: before
  committing "nothing occurs in this interval", scan the interval at the sensitivity of the
  smallest event that would refute it.
- Checking every row, not just the visually obvious one, is cheap coverage; the obvious
  pause was in fact the global maximum, but that became a verified claim rather than an
  assumption.

## Lessons

1. When an image question asks for a number, instrument the image: calibrate scale from
   many reference features and detect events programmatically; eyeball reading is the
   fallback, not the method.
2. Verify absence claims (nothing in this gap) at the amplitude threshold of the smallest
   refuting event, not at the amplitude of the events already seen.
