## RIDM 32 Application

A casually authored data-interpretation item with an expert's design: mortality and
fungal load under six pollens and a mixture for three fungi, plus egg counts with and
without infection. The data were reduced to typed facts before any option was read: two
fungi raise mortality above the uninfected baseline and one does not; one pollen clears
the first fungus and restores baseline mortality while barely touching the second; the
third fungus raises egg output, so it is a beneficial symbiont rather than a neutral
one; the most nutritious pollen loses to the clearing pollen under the first infection,
and the clearing pollen lowers output under the third. Each option was then tested
clause by clause. Every "always" clause failed against one of those facts, the
comparative-deadliness clauses failed against the mortality figures, and the option
that names the third fungus a commensal failed against the egg counts. One option
survived: the short generic statement that the first two fungi are pathogens.

The key is that option, with a rationale reducing the data to the same facts and
calling the third fungus a symbiont.

## Framework Observations

- The clause test was licensed and did the work: a superset option with one planted
  clause sat beside its all-true subset, and the subset won because the extra clause was
  false, which is the framework's generic-truth rule applied as written.
- Consumption of the givens was complete: the egg counts under the third fungus exist
  only to make the "commensal" and "always" clauses false, and the baseline mortality
  line exists only to type the third fungus.

## Lessons

- None new. Reduce constructed data to typed facts first, then kill options clause by
  clause, and let the all-true subset beat its superset with a false clause.
