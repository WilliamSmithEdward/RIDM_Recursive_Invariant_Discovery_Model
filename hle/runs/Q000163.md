## RIDM 30 Application

A constructed two-sentence passage for a self-paced reading study, ending
in a classic garden-path clause, with the question asking at which word
position elevated reading times would appear if a metonymic
interpretation headed off the later garden path. The word positions were
counted exactly. Three candidate loci were generated: the nominalized
adjective that opens the garden-path clause, read as a property standing
for the people who have it; the verb of that clause, read as a
noun-to-verb conversion; and the main clause's vehicle noun read as its
crew, with the cost of that coercion falling on the agentive verb that
forces it, in the standard processing account of predicate-driven
metonymy. The commitment took the nominalized adjective, arguing that the
context sentence primed it and that the vehicle reading was too
conventional to cost anything.

The key holds the vehicle-for-crew metonymy, with the cost located at the
agentive verb, and explains that a reader who has avoided the garden
path shows no elevated time inside the garden-path clause at all.

## Framework Observations

- Candidate coverage held again, and the keyed reading was not merely
  listed but reasoned out in the standard form, cost at the coercing
  verb, before being demoted.
- The demotion overrode a fired verdict. "Metonymic interpretation" is a
  term of art in this literature, and its curricular meaning is the
  standard figure, vehicle or place or producer standing for the people
  or product, not a nominalized adjective; the framework's rule that a
  term of art resolves to its curricular definition had selected the
  keyed reading. The override rested on a model of the author using the
  term loosely, which is a competence downgrade applied to an expert
  author, and on a domain-correctness objection (the vehicle reading is
  too conventional to cost anything) that describes the author's own
  predictable construction and so supported the rival rather than
  refuting it.
- The stem's own discriminative clue, that the garden path was "later",
  fit the far-earlier verb far better than the adjacent adjective and was
  noted without binding.
- Second consecutive run in which a fired verdict was overridden by an
  author model. The verdict ledger the gate requires was reasoned about
  in prose but never assembled as a list, and the override never had to
  name its evidence.

## Lessons

- Assemble the verdict ledger explicitly before every commitment, as a
  list of fired rules with verdicts; an override must name typed
  evidence at matching scope or it does not run. Reasoning about the
  ledger in prose is where overrides slip through.
- A term of art used by an expert author resolves to its curricular
  definition; reading it loosely to fit a preferred candidate is a
  competence downgrade, which is the failure signature the coherence rule
  already names.
