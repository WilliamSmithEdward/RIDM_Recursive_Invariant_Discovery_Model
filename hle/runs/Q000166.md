## RIDM 30 Application

A single-sentence recall item asking for the largest ship of one nation, by a named
size measure, sunk by one class of attacker within a stated period. The candidate set
held the plain answer, the largest merchant loss by tonnage, together with the only
warship of that nation lost to the attacker class in the period and a larger liner
whose wreck was torpedoed after a mine had already sunk it. The stem's size term is,
in naval usage, the measure for warships, and under that technical reading the period
qualifier does exact work by excluding a larger warship of the same nation sunk by the
same attacker class a few months after the period. That designed-detail payoff, plus
the canonical-term rule, selected the warship; the plain merchant reading was demoted
as leaving the size term imprecise and the qualifier decorative.

The key is the plain answer: the merchant tanker, with the size term used loosely for
tonnage and the qualifier read as the period during which the nation was a
belligerent. The key's rationale names the liner and excludes it on the mine, which is
the same exclusion the attempt made.

## Framework Observations

- Candidate coverage held. The keyed answer was the first-pass candidate from the
  plainest reading and was displaced by a rival whose advantage was a more designed
  reading, the displacement the framework forbids in so many words.
- The term-of-art rule was applied against provenance. On a trivia-register item the
  operative community is the general one, in which the size term means tonnage; the
  naval-precision reading is a canon transplanted from a different community. The
  previous miss ran the same rule the other way on an expert item; the discriminator
  between the two is provenance, which Section 19 already indexes canons to, and the
  attempt skipped that indexing.
- The designed-detail payoff was manufactured. The qualifier is consumed as
  scene-setting under the plain reading, the belligerent period, and scene-setting
  discharge is exactly what the framework allows for a period phrase; constructing a
  tighter payoff for the rival reading and then scoring the plain reading for lacking
  it is selection by consequence.
- Third fired-verdict override of the sprint: the first-pass protection rule and the
  canonical-reading rule both selected the key, and a sophistication argument
  overrode them.

## Lessons

- A technical term in a casually authored item resolves at the precision of the
  provenance community, and tightening it into a class selector is the same canon
  transplant as loosening an expert's term of art; the direction of the error follows
  the direction of the mismatch between the reader's register and the author's.
- A period or scope qualifier that discharges as scene-setting under the plain reading
  is not evidence for a rival reading under which it binds tightly; load-bearingness
  discriminates only when the plain reading leaves the qualifier unconsumed.
