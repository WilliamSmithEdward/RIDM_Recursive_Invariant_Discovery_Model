## RIDM 20 Application

Count fixed-length self-avoiding walks on a directed lattice with
alternating one-way streets. The only real risk was the convention layer:
which alternation pattern defines the lattice, and whether the count
depends on the starting site class. Both were handled as fork enumeration
rather than assumption: the first four terms were derived by hand as
anchors (including the two forbidden unit-plaquette closures at four
steps), the enumeration reproduced them, and the count was recomputed from
all four parity classes of starting site, which agreed, confirming
vertex-transitivity and making the mirrored-convention variant provably
equivalent. The key's published program uses the identical direction
convention, and the value matches the cited literature table.

## Framework Observations

- On convention-bearing combinatorial definitions, running the enumeration
  from every equivalence class of starting condition is a cheap way to
  convert a convention worry into a proof of invariance; here it
  discharged the only alarm on the ledger before commitment.
- Hand-derived small anchors caught nothing this time but cost seconds;
  the discipline of deriving them before trusting the enumerator is what
  makes the high band honest rather than hopeful.
