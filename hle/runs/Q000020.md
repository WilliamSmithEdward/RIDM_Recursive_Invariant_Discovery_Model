## RIDM 12 Application

A theory question asking which of five listed assumptions are required to prove a
convergence result, with the answer to be encoded in a fixed logical format. The attempt
correctly analyzed the information-theoretic structure, proved two of the listed options
individually sufficient, correctly rejected three as irrelevant, and applied the literal
reading of "must" to commit the disjunction of the two sufficient options.

The official answer is the empty set: the condition already given in the question's stem is
alone sufficient, so no listed assumption is required at all. The attempt never tested the
null candidate. Every hypothesis it evaluated was drawn from the option menu; the menu
framed the space, and the given stem condition, which was the actual engine of the proof,
was treated as background rather than as a candidate complete answer. A format example in
the task, which paired two option letters in a clause, was also over-read as weak content
evidence for a nonempty answer, when it existed only to illustrate syntax.

## Framework Observations

- Menu framing is a distinct coverage failure: for requirement-style questions, the
  candidate set must include the empty answer, and the first test is whether the stated
  givens already suffice. Sufficiency proofs for listed options say nothing about
  necessity while the null candidate is untested.
- Refinement of L16: task-text micro-evidence must be discriminative to count. Format
  examples, boilerplate, and syntax illustrations are decorative, and reading content from
  them is availability-tier inference.
- Calibration: a derived-support commitment at 55 percent missing is within expectation;
  the derivation itself was sound but aimed at a framed subspace, which is a coverage
  error, not a derivation error.

## Lessons

1. Test the null answer first in requirement-style tasks: check whether the stem's givens
   alone suffice before ranking subsets of an option menu; option lists frame and silently
   shrink the hypothesis space.
2. Only discriminative task-text features are evidence; format examples and boilerplate
   are decorative and must not sway content.
