## RIDM 14 Application

A single-sentence stem describing a comorbid clinical population and asking which
connectivity finding that population shows, with five options differing in direction,
mechanism, and laterality. The attempt classified the item as retold from one specific
study (correct: the official rationale is a single paper citation), noted the low-rigor
tell of two near-paraphrase options, and capped the band at medium. It then committed to
the direction supported by the recalled field-average literature for the relevant disorder
and substance populations against healthy controls, which also happened to be the only
option stating that direction.

The key held the opposite direction, taken verbatim from the source study. The recalled
evidence had a scope mismatch the attempt never surfaced: the field-average direction comes
from case-versus-control contrasts, while the item's population defines a within-patient
contrast, comparing patients with the comorbidity against patients without it. That
contrast is known in the dual-diagnosis literature to invert case-control expectations.
The attempt never generated the within-population contrast as a candidate frame, so the
true reading of the comparison was absent from the candidate set before ranking, a
coverage failure upstream of ranking quality.

## Framework Observations

- Scope matching applies to recalled evidence, not only retrieved evidence: the question
  is not whether a direction is well-attested but whether the attested contrast is the
  item's contrast. Who is compared with whom is part of the proposition.
- The structural-prior cap for weak recall was correctly identified and then talked past:
  generic-direction recall from a mismatched contrast felt strong but was narrative-tier
  for this proposition. When source-specific recall is absent on a retold item, the
  option-count prior binds regardless of how available a generic direction is.
- Post-reveal observation for future runs: when two options paraphrase the same finding,
  the one matching the source's lexical form is the likelier key on a retold item; the
  official answer here used the citation title's exact term while the paraphrase option
  went unkeyed.

## Lessons

1. Type the comparison, not just the claim: before reusing a recalled direction, verify
   the recalled studies' contrast (case versus control, within-population, dose response)
   matches the item's; a direction from a different contrast can invert and is
   narrative-tier for the item's proposition.
2. On retold items with no source-specific recall, the structural prior binds; and
   paraphrase-duplicate options are discriminated by verbatim match to the likely source's
   wording, not by which paraphrase reads more naturally.
