## RIDM 15 Application

A classical two-layer pavement design computation: two plate-bearing tests characterize
the subgrade and a trial section, and a deflection cap under a design wheel load fixes
the required thickness. Both candidate routes were constructed before commitment. The
canonical course route uses the rigid-plate coefficient for the tests, the
flexible-plate coefficient for the tyre, and a design chart to carry the modular ratio
from the trial section to the design case. A second route emerged from a givens audit:
the given numbers make the required deflection factor equal the trial deflection factor
to two tenths of a percent under any consistent coefficient choice, collapsing the
problem to a chart-free rescaling. The attempt read that near-equality as
reverse-engineered author intent, argued that an exact-match key needs a
chart-independent determination, and committed the chart-free result. The key followed
the canonical route with the author's own course chart and rounded the result up to a
constructible thickness; the author's engineered elegance was that the design thickness
lands next to the trial thickness, not that the deflection factors coincide.

## Framework Observations

- Third consecutive run in which the keyed route was explicitly constructed, named, and
  ranked second. The demotions came from meta-evidence: a flagged-constraint prior, a
  strictness prior, and here a numerical coincidence elevated to intent. In all three
  the standard curricular chain won.
- A discovered numerical near-coincidence among task givens is narrative-tier. Many
  ratios can be formed from a handful of given values, so near-matches arise by chance;
  treating one as decisive intent evidence is self-inflicted multiple-comparisons
  error. Only an exact identity that also selects the canonical route deserves weight.
- The determinacy argument (exact-match keys require chart-free routes) was refuted:
  the author's chart is shared with the item's original audience, and course-provenance
  items key the course's canonical procedure even when it passes through an artifact
  the solver cannot see. Provenance identification should have predicted this: the
  coefficient asymmetry is the lecture's teaching point, and the attempt noted that
  before talking itself out of it.
- Chart-memory noise made even the canonical route imprecise, but the canonical route
  plus the trial-thickness anchor would have landed within rounding of the key; the
  clever route landed far outside it.

## Lessons

1. Canonical-route precedence: on authored items, the curriculum's standard route,
   definition, or chain outranks any meta-inference about the author's design, whether
   from elegance, strictness philosophy, or numerical coincidence. Meta-inferences may
   break ties within the canonical route; they may not overturn it.
2. Numerology found in the givens is a hypothesis generator, never a discriminator: the
   space of checkable coincidences is large enough that near-equalities are expected by
   chance.
3. When an item's stated method points at an instrument the solver lacks (a design
   chart, a table), predict the key by following the canonical route through a best
   reconstruction of the instrument and prefer answers near the item's own anchors,
   such as a trial configuration, rather than switching to a route that avoids the
   instrument.
