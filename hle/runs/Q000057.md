## RIDM 16 Application

A frame-finding and translation task on a mid-ORF nucleotide sequence. The rigorous
route is purely mechanical, and the discipline applied was instrumentation of the
mechanical steps: the sequence was indexed position by position, every candidate frame
in both strands was scanned for stops, the unique clean frame was confirmed against all
five rejected frames, and each codon was translated and then re-checked against the
code table in a second pass. The reveal matched exactly. The key's rationale records
that earlier frontier models identified the correct frame but delivered translations
with wrong residues, which locates the item's real difficulty in sustained mechanical
accuracy rather than in insight.

## Framework Observations

- Long mechanical chains fail by drift, not by misunderstanding; the countermeasure is
  explicit indexing and a second verification pass structured differently from the
  first (here, chunked transcription checked against per-position numbering, then
  codons re-derived from positions rather than from the chunks).
- Checking all six frames when only one is needed converts an assumption (the clean
  frame is unique, as mid-ORF framing implies) into an observation, at trivial cost.

## Lessons

1. On items whose difficulty is sustained mechanical accuracy, spend the budget on
   redundant differently-structured verification passes rather than on speed; the
   failure mode being defended against is silent drift, which a same-structure re-read
   does not catch.
