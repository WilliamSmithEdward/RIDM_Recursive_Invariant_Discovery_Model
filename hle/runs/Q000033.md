## RIDM 14 Application

A two-stage puzzle: a riddle yields a cipher key, the key decrypts a question, and the
decrypted question must itself be answered. The riddle was solved by intersecting its two
constraints (a word class and a construction target) over small candidate sets, giving a
unique fit. Decryption under the textbook convention, where the key advances only on
letters, produced one clean English word followed by garbage. The attempt treated the
partial coherence as a localization signal: the key was right and the alignment convention
was the open variable. Enumerating implementation conventions (key advancing on every
character including spaces and punctuation, per-word key restart, running-key derivation
from a hypothesized plaintext prefix) identified the author's convention on the first
alternative, yielding a fully coherent question. The final count was performed
programmatically, with the decrypted word's length checked against its known canonical
length as an anchor.

## Framework Observations

- Encoding tasks have convention spaces, not just key spaces. Whether non-letter
  characters consume key positions is an authoring choice invisible in the task statement;
  a decryption that is partially coherent localizes the defect to the convention layer
  rather than the key layer, converting an apparent refutation into a one-variable search.
- The riddle solution needed no separate validation: the decryption producing coherent
  English is a bound-plus-witness style internal check that certifies key and convention
  jointly. Full-stimulus coherence (Section 24) did the selecting.
- Instrumented counting on a long uniform string (Section 39) removed the transcription
  risk that an eyeball count of a 45-letter word invites; the length anchor confirmed the
  decrypted word was the canonical dictionary form and not a near-variant.

## Lessons

1. When a derived key partially decodes a stimulus, treat the encoder's implementation
   conventions as the hypothesis space before discarding the key; enumerate them the way
   candidate readings are enumerated.
2. A staged puzzle validates itself forward: each stage's output earning coherence at the
   next stage upgrades both stages' support; commit only when the final stage's answer is
   instrumented, not visually counted.
