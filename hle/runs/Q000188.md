## RIDM 32 Application

A well-posed item restating a known puzzle: a player may open any proper subset of
infinitely many boxes and must guess one closed box, may use the axiom of choice, and
is asked whether a strategy succeeds with a stated probability under an eventually-zero
assumption and under no assumption. The construction was carried out rather than
recalled as a slogan: representatives of the eventually-equal classes, a partition of the
indices into ten infinite subsequences, a uniformly random choice of one, the other nine
opened to fix the largest index at which they disagree with their representatives, the
chosen subsequence opened beyond that index to fix its class, and a guess at the next
box from its representative. At most one subsequence can be the unique last to agree
with its representative, so the guess fails on at most one of ten choices, for every
configuration. The unrestricted case implies the restricted one, so both hold.

The key is both, with the same construction and the remark that the restricted case
needs no choice.
