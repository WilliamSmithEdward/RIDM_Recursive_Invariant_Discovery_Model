## RIDM 29 Application

A plane wave at normal incidence on a slab whose permittivity and
permeability share one linear time dependence, with the field
amplitude at the far face asked as a function of slab length. The
item was typed well-posed. The rigorous route ran first: with the
two material functions equal, the impedance is constant and equal
to vacuum's, so no reflection arises at either face or from the
temporal variation, and the product of the index with each field
obeys the vacuum wave equation in a stretched time coordinate. The
continuity condition at the entry face then gives the field at the
exit face as the entry amplitude scaled by the ratio of the index
at entry time to the index at exit time, and for a linear index
that ratio is an exponential in slab length over the vacuum speed,
independent of the offset constant and of time.

The givens audit flagged the offset constant as unconsumed by the
amplitude. Under the derivation it is genuinely absent from the
amplitude while controlling the phase and the exit frequency, which
the item did not ask for; the alarm discharged by explanation
rather than by charity. A closed-world anchor was run before
commitment: a one-dimensional finite-difference simulation with the
time-varying material parameters reproduced the predicted exit
amplitude to about one percent for three parameter sets, including
a decreasing index, and showed no reflected wave beyond the
initial-condition artifact.

The key's derivation is the same construction, stated through the
wave equation with time-dependent coefficients and an inverse
function for the stretched time.

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: on a physics item whose

conserved product), derive through the invariant, then anchor the
