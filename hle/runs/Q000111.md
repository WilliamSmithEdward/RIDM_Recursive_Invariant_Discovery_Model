attached. The item was classified casually authored from checkable
structure: the stem asks a compound question whose second half no option
can express, and two options overlap at genus versus species level. The
decisive move was frame selection on the image: bright echogenicity along
the non-dependent wall with dirty shadowing, read jointly with the
positional label, keys the gas-forming diagnosis rather than the ordinary

an etymology trap (a species literally named for gas production) and a

ordinary entity's number-one organism is absent, which refutes the plain
reading by menu-mismatch.

## Framework Observations

- The absent canonical organism was treated as evidence about the intended
  frame, not as a defective menu: a menu that omits the plain reading's
  obvious answer while containing the rival frame's canonical answer is
  coherent only under the rival frame. This is the menu-mismatch discipline
  run in reverse, and it converted a defect suspicion into frame support.
- Distractor-construction analysis (why would the author plant a
  gas-named species?) typed two traps and strengthened the committed
  reading, per constructed-reading demotion: each demoted rival got the
  reading that would key it, and each reading failed on canonical
  precedence or load-bearingness of the image.
- A population-level sex contrast opposing the committed diagnosis was
  kept at the distribution level per Control Law 58 and did not demote the
  designed-detail evidence.
- Scene-setting givens (admission workup, comorbidity list, the mechanism
  of the fall) were partitioned to the diagnosis layer and not
  double-counted at option selection.

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: when a stem asks for the causative
organism and the menu omits the default reading's canonical answer while
containing a rival frame's canonical answer plus name-association traps,
the menu's architecture itself identifies the intended frame.
