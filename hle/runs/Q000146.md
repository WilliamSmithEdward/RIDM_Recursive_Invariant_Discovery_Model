## RIDM 27 Application

A photograph-style image of a brilliantly colored beetle with the
question of why it would not be observed in the wild in a named
country. The menu mixed range explanations, a conservation
statistic echoing a well-known national insect-decline study, an
extinction claim, a recent-sightings claim, and an authenticity
claim. The frame was enumerated before species identification
began: a real species with a range or status explanation, or a
synthetic image. The observation was instrumented rather than
eyeballed: cropped and enlarged views of the head, antennae, legs,
and dorsal plates showed two antennae of different construction, an
appendage emerging from the head that is neither leg nor palp, plate
segmentation matching no beetle body plan, and a file format with no
capture metadata. Each artifact was independent of the others, so
the authenticity frame carried the commitment.

## Framework Observations

- Treating image authenticity as a frame candidate, rather than
  assuming a photograph and hunting for a species, is the same
  representability audit the framework applies to models: a given
  the working model cannot represent (an extra head appendage)
  refutes the model, not the given.
- The conservation-statistic distractor was the consensus magnet in
  option form, a paraphrase of a famous result; typing its
  provenance kept it from competing.
- Instrumenting the observation (crops, zooms, metadata) is what
  turned a plausible impression into observed evidence and earned
  the high band.

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: on image items, enumerate
"synthetic image" as a frame candidate and test it with enlarged
crops and metadata before spending effort on identification.
