## RIDM 17 Application

An extremal-value item over all instances of a hypothesis class: the smallest
possible size of a limit of a surjective, nonempty-valued diagram over a directed
index. The adversarial layer is a strong pull toward the wrong invariant: the
well-known countable and finite cases force nonemptiness, and that availability
suggests the floor is one. The candidate-coverage rule that includes the null and
extreme candidates put the empty limit on the table, and the commitment then
followed the closed-world validation ladder: zero is trivially a lower bound, and
a witness system achieving it was constructed and checked step by step
(directedness of the index, nonemptiness of each stage, surjectivity of each
transition map, and the cardinality contradiction that kills every thread). The
named-theorem recall for this phenomenon served as an anchor only; the
construction carried the claim.

- Bound-plus-witness was the whole run: neither half alone would have justified

## Lessons

1. For smallest-possible or largest-possible questions over a hypothesis class,
