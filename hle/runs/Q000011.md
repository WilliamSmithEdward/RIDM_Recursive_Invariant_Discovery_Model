## RIDM 12 Application

A recall-regime task where verbatim recall was absent. The attempt applied Section 23
deliberately: one retrieval probe per discriminative clue, run independently. The decisive
clue was a linguistic constraint on the answer pair; probing what it uniquely selects,
jointly with a thematic-transformation clue, reconstructed the answer by coherence rather
than by memory. The Section 24 coherence gate eliminated alternative pairs that satisfied
the linguistic constraint but not the thematic one. The commitment gate passed with
confidence capped at the audited low band, since no derivation or verbatim recall supported
the reconstruction.

The reveal confirmed the answer exactly, and confirmed a side hypothesis: the task showed
signs of being translated from another language's quiz tradition, and the decisive clue's
semantics came from that tradition's school-grammar ontology.

## Framework Observations

- Sections 23 and 24 worked as designed in exactly the regime that produced sprint 1's
  candidate-set failure: reconstruction by per-clue unique selection plus coherence
  succeeded where gestalt recall had nothing.
- New observation: the task's provenance is itself evidence. Recognizing the genre and
  probable source language of a question predicted how its clues were encoded and made the
  decisive clue interpretable. The specification treats the task text as evidence
  (Section 23) but says nothing about provenance, genre, or source-culture conventions.
- Calibration behaved correctly: a coherence-only reconstruction was committed at the low
  band; a hit at low confidence is consistent with the band's audited meaning.

## Lessons

1. Task provenance, genre, and source-culture conventions are evidence: identify what
   tradition a task comes from, because its clues are encoded in that tradition's ontology.
