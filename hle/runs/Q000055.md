## RIDM 16 Application

A meeting-time problem for two simultaneous random walkers, asking an expected value,
its variance, an odd-dimension analogue, and a general inequality. The parity invariant
disposed of the odd case, and the stem's clause about midpoint encounters was consumed
as the author's own flag for that trap. The even case reduced to a birth-death chain;
expectation and variance were derived by descent decomposition with a geometric anchor
at the boundary state, computed in exact arithmetic, and the expectation was
cross-checked by an independent full linear solve. The inequality was verified exactly
for all small even dimensions and asymptotically. Three of four components matched the
key, including the expectation to the exact fraction. The variance differed, and the
post-reveal check was decisive: the key's second moment equals the attempt's plus
exactly twice the expectation, the precise signature of using the departing state's
expected time where the landing state's belongs, an identity proved symbolically and
confirmed to exact fraction equality against the key's own published fraction. Under
the standing ruling the run records as strict incorrect with the defect documented
here.

## Framework Observations

- Second consecutive run graded against a key with a demonstrable internal defect, and
  the second where the defect was established by reproducing the key's number exactly
  from the correct value plus an identified error mechanism, the strongest available
  form of oracle-defect evidence.
- Instrumented exact arithmetic (Section 39) did its job twice: it made the attempt's
  own value reproducible by two independent routes, and it converted the post-reveal
  dispute into a fraction-equality demonstration rather than dueling decimals. The
  boundary-state anchor validated the variance recurrence before commitment.
- Referent noise scales with answer componentry: a compound exact-match answer
  multiplies the author's manual computation surface as well as the solver's. Band
  policy currently prices referent noise flat per item; multi-component numeric items
  deserve a wider allowance, since one slipped component fails the whole tuple.
- The permissive-instrumentation reading of the closed-book rule (own-model
  computation allowed, external knowledge forbidden) earned its keep and should be
  written down explicitly.

## Lessons

1. On multi-component numeric items, referent risk compounds: cap confidence below the
   single-value level even when every component is independently well-supported,
   because the key's author computed each component by hand too.
2. When a revealed key disagrees with a twice-derived value, difference the two
   numbers first: a defect mechanism often reveals itself as an exact algebraic offset,
   and reproducing the key's number from the correct one plus the mechanism settles
   authority decisively.
