## RIDM 13 Application

A symmetry-breaking counting question with a ten-option menu showing clear low-rigor tells:
one option duplicated verbatim, vague stem phrasing, and several options that are true
statements about the wrong sector. The attempt derived the standard counting (total,
unbroken, and broken generators), audited every option against it, and committed to the only
option expressing the broken-sector count correctly under the stem's own gauge-theory
framing.

The official answer is the duplicated option, and its rationale derives the same broken
count the attempt derived, then splits it by an argument that contradicts the standard
treatment the stem's framing invokes. The attempt noticed the low-rigor tells before
committing, even labeling the item as likely generated, and still attached a high band to a
key prediction on an item whose author demonstrably reasons unlike the textbook. The
duplicated option was itself weak evidence of the generator's favored answer and was read
only as noise.

## Framework Observations

- Item rigor is a measurable property with observable tells: duplicated or overlapping
  options, category-confused distractors, phrasing that names a concept without a
  well-formed question. On low-rigor items the key tracks the author's reasoning process,
  not the field's consensus, so the derivation-to-key map breaks in a characteristic way:
  the key shares the derivation's first steps and then diverges by one flawed move.
- This is the sprint's third high-band miss, and all three share one mechanism: internal
  validation certified the attempt's own reasoning while the decisive claim was a
  prediction of an author's key. The audited meaning of the high band does not survive
  transfer from well-posed items to low-rigor, retold, or casually authored ones.
- Anomalies in the answer structure (a duplicated option) are evidence about the
  generating process and belong in the ledger as such, distinct from menu-structure
  meta-reasoning about intended design, which remains narrative-tier.

## Lessons

1. Assess item rigor before setting the band: on items with generated or casually authored
   tells, cap confidence at medium regardless of derivation strength, because the key's
   author may not follow the derivation.
2. When a low-rigor item's key is being predicted, model the author's most probable
   reasoning chain, including its likely single flawed step, rather than the correct
   chain alone.
