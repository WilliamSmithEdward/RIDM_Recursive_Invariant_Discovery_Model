## Framework Observations

- The claim-type discipline predicted the reliability ordering exactly. The one claim that
  turned out partly wrong after reveal was the reported literature attribution, which had been
  kept off the critical path. The derived chain and the anchored recall both held.
- Section 34's validation ladder (direct observation, tests, static checks, and so on) has no
  rung that exists in a closed-world task. The strongest available checks were convergent
  independent derivations and anchor cases. The specification is silent about both.
- Route convergence is weaker than true independence, since both routes come from the same
  reasoner, but it is the best available internal evidence and deserves explicit standing.

## Lessons

1. Add a closed-world rung to the validation ladder: convergent independent derivation routes
   plus anchor cases with known outcomes.
2. Make recall a first-class evidence source with a stated default type (reported), and name
   the upgrade paths: derivation, anchor agreement, or external observation.
3. Anchor testing (calibrate the method on a known case before trusting it on the unknown)
   deserves a named place in the specification.
