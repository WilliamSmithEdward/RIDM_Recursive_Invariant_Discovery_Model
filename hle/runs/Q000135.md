## RIDM 26 Application

A what-can-you-infer item over simplicial-complex signals, with a
none-of-the-above option. The decisive move was recognizing that
both stated findings can be information-free: the scalar total
divergence of any edge signal vanishes identically for a signed
incidence matrix, and a search that finds no nonzero-sum cycles
cannot distinguish zero sums from the absence of cycles. Explicit
witnesses (a path with a strictly increasing vertex signal, a
constant-signal triangle) then refuted each substantive option as
a necessary inference.

The rival literal parse of the stated matrix product would have
made the finding genuinely informative, but under it two options
became simultaneously true, breaking the single-choice menu.
Menu coherence selected the vacuity reading as the only one under
which the item is well-formed, and the pool filter corroborated
it: reference models chain straight to the tempting substantive
option. The filter was used as corroboration of a constructed
reading, not as the primary selector, applying the previous run's
lesson within the same sprint.

- The key's rationale is the same construction family as the

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: for inference-menu items,
check each stated finding for vacuity before chaining it, refute
options with explicit witnesses rather than intuitions, and let
single-answer well-formedness select among notation parses.
