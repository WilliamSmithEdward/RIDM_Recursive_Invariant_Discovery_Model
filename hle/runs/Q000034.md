## RIDM 14 Application

A short clinical vignette with garbled laboratory phrasing and an odd imaging description,
correctly classified as casually authored with the band capped at medium. The attempt ran

readings was generated during coverage (for a different candidate structure) and pruned

## Framework Observations

- The unused-given audit has a dual: the absent-canonical-given audit. A candidate
  diagnosis whose vignette-mandatory features are missing from the stem loses standing,
  and on casually authored items the author often intends the absence as the discriminator.
- Stem enrichment is a claim-typing failure: filling in an unstated fact from a plausible
  association (comorbidity implying an exposure history) enters an assumed claim into the
  chain as if reported by the stem.
- Two runs this sprint have now keyed inside an option pair the attempt itself flagged as
  related (a paraphrase pair, a teaching-contrast pair) while the attempt committed
  outside the pair. Pair structure remains narrative-tier evidence, but a flagged pair
  deserves an explicit reading that would key each member before the pair is abandoned.
- A causal-consumption test discriminates readings sharing a given: the mimic reading had
  the imaging finding causing the reflux; the malignancy reading merely correlated with
  it. Readings that consume a given causally explain the stem more tightly.

## Lessons

1. Audit candidate diagnoses for absent mandatory features, not only for consumed givens;
   in authored vignettes the withheld canonical feature is often the intended
   discriminator.
2. Never let an inferred enrichment of the stem (a fact the stem could have stated but did
   not) carry support for a leading candidate; type it as assumed and test the candidates
   without it.
