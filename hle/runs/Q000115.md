## RIDM 22 Application

A yes/no conceptual item on whether one axiom of a famous two-postulate
system is deducible from the other. Both verdicts were generated with
their supporting readings: the consensus verdict (not deducible; the
rival kinematics satisfies the first postulate while violating the
second, and the derivation-from-one-postulate tradition leaves the
invariant speed's finiteness undetermined) and the
named-school verdict (deducible; the invariant-speed structure emerges
from the first postulate alone, and tying it to a specific massless
signal is an empirical add-on, so the second postulate as stated is
unnecessary). The exact literature the key draws on was identified
pre-reveal. The commitment then ranked the consensus verdict on a
population prior about expert reviewers avoiding contested positions,
and it lost: the key holds the named-school verdict, with the identified
derivation as its rationale.

## Framework Observations

- The decisive error was demoting an instance-level signal with a
  population-level prior. The survival-filter argument was explicitly
  generated pre-commitment: frontier models answer the consensus
  verdict, so the item survives an adversarially filtered pool only if
  the key sits on the other side of the split. That is typed evidence
  about this item's key, and it was charitably discharged in favor of a
  generic claim about reviewer conservatism. Alarm dismissal in
  referent-prediction form.
- On binary conceptual items with a live scholarly split, the model-
  failure filter is part of the item's provenance: the pool's
  construction rule is evidence about the key's side. The consensus
  verdict is exactly what the filtered models produce, which is what
  makes it the magnet.
- The debunking-flavored stem framing ("is it true that...") was read as
  favoring the negative verdict; that was narrative-tier and should have
  carried no weight.
- What worked: both verdicts were constructed with their keying
  readings (Control Law 59 compliance), and the key's school and
  derivation were identified precisely; the failure was pure ranking.

## Lessons

Candidate for the sprint file: on items whose answer sits on a
contested verdict with literature on both sides, predict what the
filtered frontier models would answer, and treat the item's survival in
an adversarially filtered pool as instance-level evidence for the
opposite side. A population prior about reviewers or authors cannot
demote that signal, and a consensus verdict is the planted magnet
precisely because the consensus is what the filtered models reproduce.
