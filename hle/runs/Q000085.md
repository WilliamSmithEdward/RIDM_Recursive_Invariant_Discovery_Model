## RIDM 19 Application

A question about whether tensor powers of two graphs equivalent under a
fixed dimension of the Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm can ever become
distinguishable at that dimension. The menu offered several finite cutoffs
tied to the dimension or the graph sizes, plus an all-powers option. The
route: the tensor product is the categorical product of graphs, so
homomorphism counts into a power are powers of homomorphism counts, and
the equivalence class of the algorithm at a fixed dimension is exactly
equality of homomorphism counts from a fixed graph family (the known
bounded-treewidth characterization). Equal counts raised to the same power
stay equal, so equivalence survives every tensor power; the premise about
the next dimension distinguishing the pair only certifies non-isomorphism.
The convention question (which treewidth class matches which dimension
numbering) was noted and discharged: the argument needs only that some
fixed family characterizes the dimension, so any off-by-one shift is
harmless.
