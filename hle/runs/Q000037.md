## Framework Observations

- Author-model complexity must match item rigor. Coherence-maximizing interpretation
  (choose the reading under which every condition is purposeful) is the right criterion
  for competent authors and precisely the wrong one for incoherent items, where the
  incoherence is itself evidence that conditions are not purposeful. On such items the
  leading chain is the shallowest arithmetic the coined terms can bear, with the fewest
  repairs to the author's text.
- Paired with the sprint's first run, the two author-model misses bracket the same
  parameter from opposite sides: one attributed error to a rigorous author, the other
  attributed depth to a shallow one. The author-competence estimate is a claim like any
  other and takes its support from observable rigor tells, not from charity or suspicion.
- What worked: the literal route ran first and its under-determination was proven, not
  assumed; the keyed answer was generated as a candidate; the band cap held. The failure
  was confined to the ranking criterion inside an otherwise correctly sequenced protocol.

## Lessons

1. Rank authored-key candidates by a rigor-matched criterion: on incoherent or generated
   items, prefer the chain requiring the least domain mastery and the fewest text repairs,
   with count-to-exponent arithmetic as the archetype; reserve
   every-condition-is-purposeful ranking for items whose other conditions already read as
   purposeful.
2. Treat a repair of the author's text (reading a vacuous condition as a substantive one)
   as costly evidence against a candidate reading, not as support for it; the author who
   needed the repair never made it.
