## RIDM 12 Application

Open-ended recall with genuinely failed retrieval. The attempt ran per-clue probes
(Section 23), ranked a candidate set by coherence with the campaign's theme, committed the
top pair at a confidence near the open-ended structural prior, and said so plainly.

The reveal showed one element of the committed pair was right, and it was the element the
thematic probe selected directly. The second element was wrong for a structural reason: both
slots of the two-part answer were drawn from a single candidate pool, on an unexamined
assumption that the pair shared a category. The true pair was deliberately heterogeneous,
spanning two distant domains, which is itself a common design pattern when an ensemble is
meant to signal breadth or respectability.

## Framework Observations

- The per-clue probe method partially transfers to open-ended recall: it recovered the
  element the clue actually selected. The miss came from candidate-set structure, not from
  ranking.
- Compound answers need per-slot coverage. Section 23 says widen along every dimension the
  clues span, but it does not warn against coupling the slots of a multi-part answer.
  Ensembles chosen by institutions are often diverse by design; assuming co-category
  silently narrows the set for every slot after the first.
- Calibration held: a very-low-band commitment missing is the expected outcome for the band.

## Lessons

1. Decouple compound-answer slots: generate and rank candidates for each element of a
   multi-part answer independently, and check whether the ensemble was assembled for
   diversity rather than similarity.
