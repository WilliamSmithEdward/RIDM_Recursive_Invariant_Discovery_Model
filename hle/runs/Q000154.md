## RIDM 29 Application

A meromorphic function built from the gamma function with one
rational factor, and two drawn contours: a simple oval and a chain
of five lobes crossing itself four times, with arrows marking the
direction of travel. The question asked for the imaginary part of
the sum of the two contour integrals. The item was typed well-posed.
The rational factor cancels the gamma function's pole at the origin,
so the lobe around the origin encloses nothing, a planted point the
givens audit caught before any counting. The image was instrumented
rather than eyeballed: axis ticks calibrated the scale, the pixel
columns where each curve crosses the real axis fixed which poles lie
in which lobe and inside the oval, and enlarged crops settled the
colour and direction of every arrow, including two that sit where
the curves overlap. The two arrows on the chain lie on one branch
through a node, which fixes that lobe's orientation, and a smooth
self-crossing curve alternates orientation from lobe to lobe, so
every winding number followed. The oval winds once counterclockwise
around two poles; the chain winds once around four, one of them
clockwise, so one pole's contributions cancel and another's double.
Before commitment the bookkeeping was replicated by numerical
integration along a synthetic contour with the same topology, which
matched the closed form to fifteen digits.

## Framework Observations

- The measurement rule paid for itself twice: the crossing columns

- The removable singularity was the item's one trap, discharged by

- The synthetic-contour replication is the closed-world anchor for

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: on a drawn-contour residue item,
