## RIDM 29 Application

A count of homeomorphism classes of compact spaces containing a
dense copy of the long ray such that every bounded continuous
function on the ray extends uniquely. The frame was the whole
item. Under a Hausdorff hypothesis the extension property is the
universal property of the Stone-Cech compactification, which for
the long ray is its one-point compactification, giving exactly one
class; that is the answer the reference population converges on.
The stem says only compact, and its word "unique" is redundant
under the Hausdorff reading, where density already forces
uniqueness, and load-bearing without it. Both frames were computed
to commitment quality. Without Hausdorff, twin points can be
adjoined to the closed long ray: doubling any point, or attaching a
family of mutually indistinguishable points of any cardinality to
the endpoint, keeps the space compact, keeps the ray dense, and
keeps every extension unique because each new point lies in the
closure of a punctured neighbourhood of the point it shadows. So
the literal answer is infinitely many, indeed a proper class. The
commitment named that answer and scoped the Hausdorff case as a
remark rather than hedging between the two.

The key holds "infinite" with the same endpoint-splitting
construction, and records the Hausdorff case as the one-class
alternative.
