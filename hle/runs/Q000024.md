## RIDM 13 Application

A long argument-shaped stem about assigning ortholog versus paralog status to two duplicated
genomic elements, with a two-panel figure, twenty answer options including "more than one is
correct" and "none are correct", and several mirror-paired distractors. The attempt correctly
localized all predicted coding segments to one element (eliminating a family of placement
options, which the official rationale confirms), correctly concluded the elements are
separate genes, and correctly rejected the conflict-claiming and agnostic options. It then
committed the orthology assignment in the wrong direction.

Three process failures produced the miss. First, the stem explicitly directed the reader to
the figure for a decisive count ("see the figure for the number..."); the attempt never
performed that observation before committing, and the count contradicts the direction it
chose. Second, the stem's own nickname for one element (a "potential X-like region" framing)
was treated as weak evidence for that element's orthology; a label in the stem is
presentation, not evidence. Third, the longest paragraph in the stem, a failed search for a
conserved structural feature in the labeled element, was classified as neutral detail when
its argumentative function was refutation: the feature's absence made that element the worse
structural match. Post-reveal, a menu-structure heuristic (reading mirror-paired options as
design hints) also contributed; it is narrative-tier and ranked the two live finalists the
wrong way.

Dual-track note (Section 48): the stem states a count in prose that is in tension with the
count the official rationale reads off the figure; the figure observation supports the key's
direction, so this is logged as a hard miss with a documented stem-prose tension, not as a
defective key.

## Framework Observations

- An explicitly flagged observable is part of the task contract. When the task says the
  figure carries a specific quantity, that quantity is decisive by declaration, and
  committing without observing it is an unforced grounding failure.
- In an argument-shaped stem, each costly block of presented evidence exists to move the
  conclusion somewhere. Before ranking answers, assign each block a direction of force; a
  long negative-result passage is usually a refutation aimed at the most available reading.
- The stem's labels, nicknames, and framing sentences are presentation, in the same class as
  option menus under Control Law 25. The labeled hypothesis is the one to stress-test, not
  the one to inherit.
- Menu meta-reasoning (mirror pairs, distractor architecture) is availability-tier support
  and may not rank live candidates.

## Lessons

1. Treat task-flagged observables as contract: enumerate every "see the figure for X"
   pointer and observe each X before the commitment gate.
2. Compute the direction of force of every costly evidence block in an argument-shaped
   stem; a detailed failed-search narrative is evidence against the hypothesis it probed.
3. Stem labels and question framing are presentation, not evidence; menu architecture is
   not evidence either.
