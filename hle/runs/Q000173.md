## Framework Observations

- The derivation's governing model was unsupported and it outranked matched recall. The
  rake adjustment was an estimate of a solver's output from a hand-built equity sketch,
  an inference whose model validity was assumed, and the framework already says such a
  derivation does not outrank recall matched to an identified source in an empirically
  governed regime. The recall was the canonical reading; the adjustment was sophistication
  spent against it rather than on proving its consequences.
- A phrasing alarm was dismissed. Asking for the worst hand of a row that one should open
  presupposes an interior cutoff; if the whole row opened, the question would be empty.
  That alarm was noticed, explained away as a possible trick, and left unbound. Under the
  committed reading it never discharged, and binding it would have re-ranked toward the
  rival that explains it, which is the key.
- Candidate coverage held and the band was right. The keyed hand was the first-pass
  candidate and the miss was priced low, but the low band does not excuse the ranking
  error: the plain first-pass candidate was displaced by a more designed reading, the
  displacement the framework names.

## Lessons

- A stem that asks for the boundary element of a set presupposes that the boundary lies
  inside the set; a reading under which the whole set qualifies leaves the question empty
  and is refuted by that emptiness unless the stem licenses the trivial case.
- An adjustment to a recalled empirical result, argued from a hand-built model of the
  instrument that produced the result, is an inference at the decision step and cannot
  displace the recalled value; it prices a rival and caps the band.
