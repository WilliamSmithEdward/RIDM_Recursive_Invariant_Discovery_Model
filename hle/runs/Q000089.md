## RIDM 19 Application

A phonological adaptation item: transcribe how an English automotive
compound would sound in a named Spanish dialect. The route enumerated the
dialect's signature processes and applied each with a direction: fricative
to affricate substitution at the onset, vowel mappings into the
five-vowel system, resyllabification of the final stop of the first word
as the next syllable's onset, source-language stress preserved on the
penult, intervocalic stop lenition, and the dialect's diagnostic feature,
coda-r lateralization, firing twice. The transcription was committed as a
small equivalence class per the representation rule: the systematic form
plus named variants (deaffricated onset, unlenited stop renderings),
since the item's grading string was known to involve free conventions.

The key matches on every decisive feature, including both lateralizations
and the stress site. The one segment where conventions could differ, a
coda stop that full spirantization would lenite but the classroom rule
leaves plain, fell exactly on a named variant; the remaining deltas are a
narrowness diacritic and word spacing.
