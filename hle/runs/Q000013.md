## RIDM 12 Application

The attempt identified the correct literature result, the correct authors, and the correct
mechanism, and even sketched the derivation to the right closed form: the answer is a
log-ratio of two computable quantities. It then committed a constant recalled from the
literature instead of finishing the computation.

The recalled constant was correct for the source's parameterization and wrong for the
task's: the question had deliberately altered one parameter, and its author states this was
done precisely to defeat constant-copying. Substituting the task's parameter into the
already-sketched closed form, a ten-minute computation, yields the official answer exactly.

## Framework Observations

- Recalled results carry their parameterization silently. Section 19 lists scope match as an
  evidence dimension, but nothing forced a scope-match check between the remembered
  constant's parameter binding and the task's parameters. The mismatch was checkable and
  unchecked.
- The commitment gate's dominance check failed in a subtler way than sprint 1's version: the
  stronger-typed alternative (finish the derivation) was not a competing answer but a
  competing route, and its cost was overestimated. When a derivation route is feasible at
  comparable cost, it dominates recall of a numeric constant.
- Benchmark provenance (L18) should have raised the prior that the task was engineered
  against memorization; the altered-parameter trap is a standard anti-recall design.
- Calibration: the medium band is now 0 for 5 lifetime. Unverified numeric recall belongs in
  the low band by rule, not by feel.

## Lessons

1. Scope-match recalled constants: before reusing a remembered numeric result, verify the
   task's parameters match the source's binding; assume hard tasks alter parameters to
   defeat copying. When the derivation to the constant is feasible, compute instead of
   copying, and let the recalled value serve only as an anchor check.
