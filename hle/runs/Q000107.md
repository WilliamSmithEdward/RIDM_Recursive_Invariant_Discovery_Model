## RIDM 21 Application

A deterministic simulation task with a bespoke tape notation. The
transcript notation was anchored on the stem's own worked example before
simulating (right-edge blank rendering, edge blanks included only while
the head sits on them, head marker before the current symbol), the
machine was simulated by hand, and the full transcript was then
reproduced by a short script implementing the notation rules exactly.
The two routes agreed character for character, and the committed string
matched the key identically.

## Framework Observations

- The key's own rationale says most models fail here on the starting head
  position or by silently growing the tape. Both failure modes are
  representation-layer errors, and both were closed by the same two moves:
  consuming the stem's format example as a flagged observable (the
  convention test cases), and instrumenting the observation with a
  mechanical reproduction instead of trusting a hand trace alone.
- Convergent-route validation (Section 40) at trivial cost: when a task is
  mechanically checkable, the check is cheaper than the confidence
  argument it replaces.

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: on bespoke-notation simulations, parse
the notation from the stem's own examples first, then verify the full
output mechanically before commitment.
