## Framework Observations

- This is a Section 30 representation fork, exactly the class the fork
  enumeration rule exists for: a sign convention on a defined symbol. The
  commitment did enumerate a fork, but a fake one: two algebraic
  rearrangements of the same expression, which cover nothing. The real fork
  was the semantic sign of the defined quantity (thermodynamic potential
  versus positive expenditure), and it was live: the stem's definition
  sentence supports both readings, and the author's rationale confirms the
  expenditure reading.
- The fork was detectable before commitment: under the standard sign
  convention the formula needs the leading minus to make the stated
  positive plateau values come out, and an author writing "simple formula"
  may absorb that minus into the symbol's definition. A one-line check of
  which sign convention makes the author's requested expression positive
  would have surfaced both variants for a scoped dual commitment.
- What worked: phase-pair selection against the farther-stage rival (the
  named weakest link held), convergent two-route derivation of the
  common-tangent coefficients, and the consistency solve that rejected an
  earlier false inconsistency by separating single-phase marginals from
  coexistence potentials.

## Lessons

Candidate for the sprint file: when a stem defines a symbol and asks for a
formula in it, the symbol's sign semantics is a representation fork of the
Section 30 kind. Enumerate the convention that makes the requested quantity
come out positive alongside the standard one, and commit one claim with
both bookkeepings scoped. An enumerated fork must span semantically
distinct conventions; rearrangements of one expression are not coverage.
