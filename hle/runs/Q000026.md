## RIDM 13 Application

A binary design question: given measured costs for floating-point operations, integer
operations, and function calls, is the given recursive algorithm already the fastest, or can
the slower-looking direct integer alternative be combined with it? The naive comparison
makes the recursive algorithm win by an order of magnitude, which would trivialize the
question. The attempt instead audited the givens for unused parameters: the function-call
cost and the low-precision license have no role in the naive comparison, so their presence
signals the intended analysis, a crossover hybrid. Modeling the hybrid (recurse to a cutoff,
then switch to the direct integer method) showed it beating the pure recursion under every
consistent reading of the cost model, with an optimal cutoff around sixteen; the official
rationale's construction and optimal cutoff matched exactly.

The prior run's lesson was applied deliberately: the commitment band was set by the weakest
decisive link (the inference about the author's intended comparison), not by the arithmetic,
which was checked and robust.

## Framework Observations

- Unused givens are direction-of-force evidence. A measured cost that plays no role in the
  surface comparison exists to power a deeper one; enumerating which givens each candidate
  answer consumes is a cheap coverage probe. This is the constructive twin of the previous
  run's lesson that every costly evidence block exerts force somewhere.
- The binary answer format hid a three-way candidate space (algorithm A, algorithm B, a
  hybrid). Control Law 25 applied: the menu of named algorithms is not the space of

- Robustness across model readings substituted for a single verified reading: the hybrid

## Lessons

1. Audit the givens: any supplied constant or condition unused by the leading reading is
   evidence for a rival reading that consumes it.
2. When an ambiguity does not change the committed answer under any plausible resolution,
   record that dominance argument instead of resolving the ambiguity.
