## RIDM 13 Application

A two-part device-physics problem built on a measurement plot: identify a material from a
derived barrier parameter, then compute a terminal voltage under a stated transport model.
The attempt read clean slope and intercept values off the plot, cross-checked them against
multiple data points, derived the doping and built-in potential, and composed the barrier
height from both required terms rather than stopping at the built-in potential alone. The
material call then required one recall step (a work-function table); three nearby candidates
were enumerated and the closest match committed. The second part followed the task's own
stated model of the parasitic junction opposing the main one, and the sign convention was
taken from the task text rather than re-derived against it.

Two RIDM 13 rules were load-bearing. The intermediate barrier value was carried at derived
precision through the exponential instead of being rounded first (Section 16); the reveal's
own intermediate current density matched to three digits. And the compound answer's slots
were supported independently: a plot-derived slot and a table-recall slot, each with its own
uncertainty (Section 23).

## Framework Observations

- Carrying derived precision through an exponential is not cosmetic: the barrier value
  enters as an exponent, and pre-rounding it moves the intermediate current density by
  several percent. The lesson from the prior sprint (precision snapping) directly paid off.
- The task text's qualitative framing sentence (which junction opposes which, what is
  reduced) settled a sign question that pure device physics leaves messier at this level of
  idealization. Section 10's rule that the task text is the contract applied to a physical
  modeling choice, not just to answer format.
- The recall slot (work-function table) was the weakest link and was flagged as such; the
  clean agreement of the derived target with one canonical tabulated value functioned as a
  coherence check on the whole chain.

## Lessons

1. When a derived physical parameter lands within rounding of one canonical tabulated
   value, that agreement is evidence the chain is right; a mid-chain error would scatter
   the target between table entries.
2. In idealized device problems, the task's qualitative model statements (which element
   opposes, what is reduced) are contract, and re-deriving the physics against them wastes
   the attempt; follow the stated idealization and note the idealization boundary.
