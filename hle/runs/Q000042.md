## RIDM 15 Application

A casually authored clinical vignette: a symptom cluster establishing a well-known
diagnosis, a menu of drug and drug-combination options, and an emphasized conditional
clause stating that the patient takes an over-the-counter analgesic, feels it helps, and
will not stop it. Rigor was classified casually authored from structure (typos, informal
register), the medium cap was applied, and the author's chain was reconstructed. Two
readings were constructed and both were in the candidate set: a coverage reading, in
which the best option is the combination whose components jointly consume every listed
symptom, and an exclusion reading, in which the retained analgesic interacts with one
drug class and eliminates every option containing it. The attempt committed on the
coverage reading. The key followed the exclusion reading: the emphasized clause was the
discriminator, and the interaction eliminated two thirds of the menu.

## Framework Observations

- Candidate coverage succeeded and ranking failed, the exact split RIDM 15 Section 23
  is designed to expose. The unused-givens audit (prefer the reading consuming all
  givens) was allowed to outrank the flagged-constraint audit (prefer the reading under
  which the emphasized twist binds hardest), and on this authored item the constraint
  was the point.
- Givens were not partitioned by role. The symptom cluster existed to establish the
  diagnosis; once consumed by the diagnosis step it should have carried no further
  weight in option selection. Crediting the answer for consuming diagnosis-layer givens
  double-counted them and diluted the one decision-layer given.
- Author-competence was underestimated on one axis: surface typos were read as
  predicting shallow pharmacology, but the tested interaction is standard curriculum.
  Language proficiency and domain knowledge are independent axes.
- Calibration: inference-backed medium commitments are now well below the medium band's
  stated meaning across the audit history. The rigor cap is a ceiling; the audited
  record argues these commitments should default lower unless menu-wide coherence is
  decisive.

## Lessons

1. Partition an authored stem's givens into diagnosis-establishing and
   decision-modifying before ranking. Diagnosis-layer givens are consumed by naming the
   diagnosis; the decision layer, often a single emphasized constraint or preference
   clause, selects the option, and a conditional sentence frame around that clause
   flags it as the discriminator.
2. When the unused-givens audit and the flagged-constraint audit disagree on an
   authored item, the flagged constraint wins: authors write the twist clause to be
   binding, and scene-setting givens do not need the answer to consume them.
3. Estimate author competence per axis: writing quality bounds neither domain
   knowledge nor the standard-curriculum rules the item can test.
