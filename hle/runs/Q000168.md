## RIDM 30 Application

The orbifold Euler characteristic of a quotient stack of smooth plane curves of a
fixed degree by the projective automorphism group of the plane. Typed well-posed. The
naive route, the Euler characteristic of the open set divided by that of the group, is
meaningless because the group's Euler characteristic vanishes, so the stack was
identified instead: smooth plane curves of that degree up to projective equivalence
are exactly the non-hyperelliptic curves of one genus with their canonical embedding,
and the stabilizers are the curves' full automorphism groups, so the quotient stack is
the complement of the hyperelliptic locus in the moduli stack of that genus. The
moduli stack's value is the classical Harer-Zagier number; the hyperelliptic locus is
a two-element gerbe over unordered branch-point configurations on the line, whose
value follows from the Euler characteristic of the configuration space of ordered
points divided by the symmetric group and by two. The same hyperelliptic formula one
genus lower reproduces the Harer-Zagier value for that genus, where every curve is
hyperelliptic, which anchored the gerbe factor. The difference gave the committed
fraction; the key computes it by exactly this decomposition.
