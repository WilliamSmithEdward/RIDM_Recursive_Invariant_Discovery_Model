## RIDM 29 Application

The shortest straight-line surface trajectory on a unit Platonic
solid from a vertex back to itself avoiding every other vertex, with
the answer demanded as a minimal polynomial. The item was typed
well-posed and computational. An unfolding tracer was built from the
solid's face graph: rays from the start vertex are followed across
faces, each crossed face is developed into the plane, and copies of
the start vertex met along the way become candidate targets, each
re-traced exactly to confirm that its first vertex contact is the
start vertex. A fine angular sweep found four symmetric copies of
one shortest trajectory and nothing shorter, and a sweep at a
four-times finer step over the shorter range confirmed the gap. Its
squared length was identified two ways: by integer search in the
golden-ratio order, forced by the observation that every developed
vertex is an integer combination of unit vectors at multiples of
thirty-six degrees, and independently by an integer-relation
algorithm. The quartic was checked for irreducibility by a norm
argument.

The key holds a different quartic, taken from a stated length in
the published figure the item was built from.

## Framework Observations

- Key quality. The key's stated squared length is not an element of
  the golden-ratio order: its coefficient on the surd is not a
  half-integer, so no vertex-to-vertex development on the unit
  solid can have that length at all. The key's own figure draws an
  auxiliary edge path along the trajectory's displacement, one
  edge, three face diagonals, two edges, and a closing edge at the
  pentagon's angles; summing those vectors gives exactly the
  squared length committed here, and the figure's horizontal and
  vertical extents match that sum to a fraction of a percent. The
  key's number is a miscalculation of the length of its own
  trajectory. The grade stays incorrect under the strict rule and
  the defective-key count stays frozen at two; the observation
  lives here only.
- The algebraic-structure check (which ring the answer must live
  in) is a cheap oracle-consistency instrument that would have
  flagged the key's number before any measurement. It is the same
  representability audit the framework applies to models, applied
  to a verdict.
- The band was right for the derivation and wrong for the referent:
  a well-posed computational item can still carry a hand-computed
  key, and the componentry allowance (a minimal polynomial has
  several coefficients the author computes by hand or by tool
  transcription) was not priced.

## Lessons

- On a computational item whose key is a hand-transcribed or
  tool-transcribed number, price a transcription allowance even at
  the high band, and pre-compute the ring or field the true answer
  must lie in so a defective verdict is refutable exactly on reveal.
