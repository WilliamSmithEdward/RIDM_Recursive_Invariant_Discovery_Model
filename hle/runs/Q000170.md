## RIDM 30 Application

A menu item asking for the maximal genus of the boundary of a compact region whose
boundary has nowhere-vanishing mean curvature vector, with options ranging from zero
through two specific values to "no upper bound". Typed well-posed and
expert-authored, with two foil values that read as generated noise. The stem's
conditions were consumed first: a connected compact boundary with nonvanishing mean
curvature has mean curvature of one sign, and the sign at the farthest point from
the origin makes the boundary strictly mean-convex. The rigorous route then ran as a
construction: a thin tubular neighborhood of a closed curve gives genus one; for
arbitrary genus, a ball carrying tangent-cone spikes joined in pairs by thin bent
tubes, where the only concave step is the flare from tube to cone. That flare was
reduced to a one-line criterion for surfaces of revolution, the profile must stay
inside the catenoid's, and an explicit flare profile was checked numerically to keep
the mean curvature positive throughout, as were the bent tubes and the cones. With
every genus attainable, the unbounded option was committed.

The key is the unbounded option; its rationale gives the same tubular-neighborhood
idea with thin connecting tubes, and reports that the reference models failed by
confusing mean-convex with convex or by misapplying a rigidity theorem about
constant mean curvature.
