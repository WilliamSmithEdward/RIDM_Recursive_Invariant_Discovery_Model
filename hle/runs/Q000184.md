## RIDM 32 Application

A one-line stem in non-native English asks how a cost is measured, with five procedural
options. Two options share a core procedure, a selection coefficient of hybrid lines
against lines without gene flow, and differ in their tail: one adds a within-line mating
control to separate the cost of meiosis from the cost of gene flow, the other adds a list
of three fitness measures. The item was typed generated-class from its grammar, which
licensed seed centrality and struck the clause test. Centrality favored the list option,
which shares phrases with more distractors; the stem's plural "ways" was read as anchoring
to a list of measures; and the first instinct, the control option, was treated as the
population's modal answer and demoted by the pool filter. The list option was committed.

The key is the control option. Its rationale is a practitioner's description of the
design: the cost or benefit of meiosis that mating itself incurs must be measured in
within-line matings and compared against the gene-flow lines before any effect is
attributed to gene flow.

## Lessons

- Non-native or awkward grammar is register, never a rigor tell. The generated class is
  assigned only from structural tells (duplicated options, category-confused distractors,
  no well-formed question); a fluent-in-content stem with an awkward surface is casual
  expert, and the clause test is licensed on it.
- The pool filter needs a converged elimination to key against. A first instinct is not
  an elimination, and using the filter to move off an instinct is a tie-break by an
  instrument fired at a strength the item's convergence never earned.
