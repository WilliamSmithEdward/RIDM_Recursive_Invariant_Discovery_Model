## RIDM 18 Application

A which-measure-will-NOT-work item about mitigating overreliance on AI
recommendations in a professional judgment setting. Four options mapped
cleanly onto the classic mitigator list from the imported domain's systematic
review (accountability, per-decision confidence display, practice, required
justification), and one option read as that list's salience mitigator copied
with its direction inverted. The committed chain: a single-key menu can hold
exactly one non-mitigator, the inverted-salience option is un-glossable as a
mitigator under the stem's own overreliance premise, and the rival key
(practice, via the complacency argument that routine use breeds trust rather
than vigilance) would require the author to read inverted salience as
helpful, so it was demoted.

The key was the practice option, on exactly the complacency chain that had
been constructed and demoted. The author's rationale glosses higher salience
as inducing deeper processing of the highlighted information, supported by
citations from the item's own domain literature rather than the imported
domain's review, and states outright that the models all chose the
inverted-salience option: the item was selected because that chain is
maximally available.

## Framework Observations

- The decisive failure was refuting a completed rival with an assumed
  universal. The rival's chain was built to parity, but its refutation rested
  on the absence claim that no supporting gloss for the alternative reading
  exists. Absence of a gloss is an assumed claim, not an observed one; the
  gloss existed in the item's own provenance community, with citations.
- Canon indexing failed at the domain-transfer boundary. The stem announced
  the transfer explicitly (a bias usually studied in one field, applied here
  to another). The direction of the salience effect was taken from the
  imported field's canon while the author graded from the destination
  field's literature. The provenance rule was applied to the wrong side of
  the transfer.
- The single-key structural argument assumed the option statuses partition
  under the solver's canon. That argument is only as strong as the canon
  choice it rests on, and it converted a canon uncertainty into false menu
  coherence, which then lifted the band two steps.
- Anti-model selection pressure was underpriced. In a pool filtered so that
  frontier models fail, the option that is the textbook inversion under the
  most available canon is the model magnet, and a defensible second-order
  effect (practice breeding complacent trust) gains prior precisely by being
  less available.

## Lessons

1. A rival demoted on the claim that no gloss could support the competing
   reading has not been refuted: absence-of-gloss is an assumed universal,
   and the probe is to search the item's own provenance community for the
   gloss before the demotion stands.
2. On items that announce a domain transfer, index the canon to the
   destination domain, the one the author works in and grades from, not the
   source domain the stem imports for framing.
3. Whole-menu coherence built on a canon choice inherits that choice's
   uncertainty; it cannot lift the band above the canon claim's own support,
   and in anti-model-filtered pools the cleanest canonical inversion on the
   menu is evidence of the trap, not of the key.
