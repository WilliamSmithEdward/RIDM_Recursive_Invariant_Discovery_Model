## RIDM Application

The computation itself was sound: all candidate algorithms were costed under the stated
model, and the analysis correctly established that a smarter algorithm, admissible under one
of the listed options, strictly dominated every named alternative. That claim was derived.

The failure happened at final selection. The task text stated a bare optimality criterion,
and the option set explicitly included an open-ended alternative. Instead of following the
derived dominance result, the attempt substituted an inferred model of the question author's
intent ("the designed lesson is probably the simpler reversal") and chose the dominated
option. An inferred claim silently outranked a derived one at the decision step.

## Framework Observations

- The grounding rule (Section 15) was violated in spirit: the material conclusion depended
  decisively on an unsupported inferred claim about intent, while a well-supported derived
  claim pointed elsewhere. Nothing in the specification currently forces claim-type
  precedence to be re-checked at the moment of final selection, after analysis is complete.
- Interpretation selection under forced choice lacks a rule for literal-contract precedence.
  When clarification is unavailable, intent modeling should only break ties among readings
  the task text supports equally; it must not override what the text derivably asks.
- The adversarial-calibration pass (lesson L5) fired but stopped one layer early. The
  question contained a shallow designed reversal and a deeper one. Finding the first trap
  created false confidence that the trap had been found.
- Confidence typing worked as intended: the stated probability was moderate and the correct
  option was explicitly named as the leading residual. The loss came from the decision rule,
  not from calibration.

## Lessons

1. Literal contract precedence: in forced-choice settings, the task text and its option set
   are the contract. Intent modeling may only break ties between readings the text equally
   supports; it cannot override a derived result under the stated criterion.
2. Trap-depth iteration: after locating one designed reversal, continue the search until no
   admissible option dominates the current choice under the literal criterion.
3. Enforce claim-type precedence at final selection: before committing, re-check that the
   chosen option is not dominated, under the stated success criterion, by an alternative
   supported by stronger-typed claims. Analysis-time discipline does not survive the decision
   step on its own.
