## RIDM 32 Application

An expert-authored vignette: a private seller repairs a specific item for a buyer who has
paid in full, tells the buyer during the repair that it is nearly done and names a pickup
date, finishes the repair, and loses the item to a flood before pickup. Five options
place the passing of risk at different moments. The governing rule was identified first:
risk follows property, and for specific goods that the seller is bound to work on,
property passes only when the work is done and the buyer has notice of that. The stem's
one engineered detail, that the notice said the work was nearly finished and named a
future date, was consumed under that rule: notice given before completion is not notice
that the thing is done. The option that keeps the money with the buyer on the ground of
inadequate notice was committed; the rival that treats a dated advance notice as
sufficient was named as the planted magnet.

The key is that option, with a rationale citing the same rule and an appellate decision
holding that advance notice of completion by a certain date is not valid notice.

## Framework Observations

- The canonical reading led and the designed detail selected among completed rivals: the
  advance-notice rival was constructed and refuted by the rule's timing requirement, not
  by a preference.
- The provenance register fixed the precision of "notice": in this statute it is a term of
  art with a timing condition, and the stem's wording was built to fail it.

## Lessons

- None new. Identify the governing rule, consume the engineered detail under it, and name
