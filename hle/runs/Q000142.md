## RIDM 27 Application

A piecewise parametric closed curve, two half-conics of different
scale joined along a common diameter, and the smallest square that
circumscribes it. The item was typed well-posed: precise statement,
self-contained criterion, orientation of the square unconstrained by
the text. The rigorous route ran first: the figure is convex, so an
enclosing square at a given orientation exists exactly when the
figure's widths along the square's two axes fit, and the support
function of the union is the piecewise maximum of the two halves'
support functions. That gave a closed-form width function, and the
side length is the minimum over orientation of the larger of two
perpendicular widths, attained where they are equal, at a diagonal
orientation. A dense numeric sweep over orientations reproduced the
closed form to nine digits.

The axis-aligned bounding box was the named magnet; it was computed
and discharged as dominated. The key's rationale reaches the same
side length by an explicit tangent-line construction.

## Framework Observations

- The item's only trap was frame defaulting, taking the square as
  axis-aligned because the curve is presented in the standard axes.
  Enumerating orientation as part of the frame, rather than as an
  afterthought, made the magnet visible and the derivation short.
- Two routes with little shared machinery (support-function algebra
  and brute-force projection) agreed, which is what earns the high
  band on a closed-world derivation.

## Lessons

No new failure lesson. Win pattern: on extremal-enclosure items,
treat the enclosure's orientation as a frame candidate, derive the
width function, and replicate numerically before commitment.
