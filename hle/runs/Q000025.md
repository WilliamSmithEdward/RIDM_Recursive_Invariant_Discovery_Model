## RIDM 13 Application

A clustering-coefficient computation on a hierarchically constructed 64-node network, where
the task text retells a canonical construction from the network-science literature in its own
words, alongside a figure. The attempt noticed early that the retold wiring differs from the
canonical model, treated that as a contract question, and resolved it by zooming the figure,
which visibly matches the retold text. It then derived the coefficient for that wiring with
three internal cross-checks (edge count by construction, by degree sum, and by direct
enumeration of neighbor edges), all agreeing.

The official answer instead carries the source publication's numbers for the canonical
example, which differ from both the retold construction's value and from the attempt's own
reconstruction of the canonical variant. The derivation was conditionally correct; the miss
was in predicting which construction the key scores. The stated high confidence was
attached to the strength of the derivation, but the decisive claim behind the commitment
was an inference about the key's referent, which deserved no more than coin-flip support.
The confidence band should have been capped by that weakest decisive link.

Scored strict incorrect by operator ruling: a divergence between a retold construction and
its source's published values is a task-reading risk, not a defective key.

## Framework Observations

- Task provenance (Section 19) cuts deeper than genre: when a task is recognizably a
  paraphrase of a specific published example, the probable answer key is the source's
  published value, and text-level deviations introduced by the paraphrase are noise until
  proven load-bearing. The attempt identified the source family, then still let the literal
  re-telling outweigh the provenance prior.
- Composite commitments inherit the band of their weakest decisive link (Sections 21 and
  29). A flawless derivation conditioned on an uncertain contract reading is an inference,
  not a derivation, at the moment of commitment. Typing the whole chain by its strongest
  segment inflated the band.
- The internal cross-checks were real but validated only the conditional branch; no check
  exercised the branch condition itself. Validation must cover the claim that selects the
  branch, not only the branch's interior.

## Lessons

1. When a task retells a recognizable published example, weight the source's published
   answer as the leading candidate and treat retelling-induced differences as noise unless
   the task explicitly signals a variant.
2. At the commitment gate, set the band from the weakest decisive link in the chain,
   explicitly including contract-reading and key-referent inferences, not just the
   computational segments.
3. When two constructions are live, compute both values and weigh them as candidate
   answers; an airtight derivation of one branch is not coverage of the candidate space.
