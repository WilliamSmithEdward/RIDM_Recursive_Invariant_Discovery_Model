## Framework Observations

- Canonical-reading precedence was applied with the wrong index. The canon that governs
  an authored item is the provenance population's canon, at its most specific the source
  paper's own usage, not the discipline-wide textbook default. The source community was
  identified early, its convention was named as a live rival, and the rival was then
  demoted in favor of the curriculum default plus a design-elegance meta-prior, the exact
  prior class Control Law 33 restricts to tie-breaking.
- The rival frame was also miscomputed before being demoted: evaluated carelessly, it
  produced a wrong answer triple, which made it easier to dismiss. A rival reading must
  be computed to the same standard as the leading one before ranking, or the ranking
  compares a finished candidate against a strawman.
- Strong-segment banding recurred: the derivations inside each convention were airtight,
  and the commitment took its band from them while the decisive link was the convention

  commitment inference-backed, which defaults low, not high.
- The scaffolded-parts assumption (authors do not ask redundant parts) is false for

## Lessons

1. Index the canon to the item's provenance: when a stem derives from or cites a specific
   paper or community, that community's terminological conventions and standing results
   are the operative referent, and they outrank the discipline's textbook default.
2. Compute every live rival to commitment quality before ranking; a rival evaluated
   sloppily and then demoted was never really in the candidate set.
3. A commitment whose decisive link is a convention or frame choice is inference-backed
   regardless of how rigorous the mathematics inside each frame is; band it accordingly.
