## RIDM 15 Application

A range question: the extreme values of a rank invariant of a symmetric power of a
curve, over the moduli of such curves. The structural step, identifying the symmetric
power as a projective bundle over the Jacobian so that the rank is the Jacobian's
Picard number plus one, matched the key's own rationale exactly, and the minimum
agreed. The maximum did not: the key asserts, as bare recall, a low upper bound for
the Picard number of the underlying abelian threefold, while classical and checkable
examples, a highly symmetric quartic curve whose Jacobian is isogenous to the cube of
a CM elliptic curve, give the standard maximal value, well above the key's bound, and
the invariant is isogeny-stable. The oracle-discipline check ran post-reveal: the
key's central recall claim is refuted by an explicit classical construction that the
mathematical literature uses for precisely this purpose, so the surprise decomposes as
a defective referent rather than a reasoning error in the attempt. Under the standing
strict-grading ruling the run records as incorrect, with the defect noted here only.

## Lessons

1. When a key contradicts a derivation, decompose the surprise before updating: a
   defect demonstrated by an explicit classical counterexample reopens nothing in the
   reasoning layer, and the model revision that would "fix" such a miss would corrupt
   the model instead.
2. Range-of-invariant questions over a moduli space are two questions, a generic value
   and an extremal value; treat the extremal side's attainment claim as its own
   decisive link, since that is where an author's recall is most likely to be shallow.
