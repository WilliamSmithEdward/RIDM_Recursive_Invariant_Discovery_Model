## RIDM 21 Application

Two-stage item: identify a named rearrangement's product, then compute a
published molecular-complexity metric on it. The product identification was
canonical and matched the key. The metric was reconstructed closed-book
from the source paper, validated against two recalled anchors (the
smallest-molecule bit value and the paper's flagship symmetry comparison),
and computed with an enumerated fork on the symmetry rule. The committed
value used hydrogen-inclusive bond and element counts, under which the
formula's exclusion clause works by making hydrogen terms vanish in the
sum.

The key computes the same metric hydrogen-blind: the exclusion clause is
applied inside the per-atom parameter definitions rather than at the sum,
so ring methylenes get two bonds instead of four and single-element
microenvironments. That variant produces a value less than half the
faithful-algorithm one. The key's arithmetic is internally consistent with
its own table, though its prose steps contradict the table on two atoms'
microenvironments (one oxygen is assigned a hydrogen it does not have, and
another's element count changes between steps). Under the hydrogen-blind
reading the smallest calibration molecule is uncomputable, which is what
originally steered the reconstruction toward hydrogen inclusion.

## Framework Observations

- The referent on compute-metric-X items is the author's operationalization
  of the algorithm, not the algorithm. The candidate set held only
  faithful-algorithm variants (three convention forks were enumerated and
  priced), and never generated the degraded variant the key used. That is
  mechanism-class myopia one level up: misreading classes of a definition
  are part of the referent space, and the most likely misreading was
  predictable, because the exclusion clause is stated once in the source
  and transplants naturally from sum scope to parameter scope.
- The low band was correct and did its job: the commitment named the
  algorithm-convention link as fragile before the reveal.
- The symmetry-rule fork enumerated at commitment turned out to be
  numerically moot for this molecule (half-weighting duplicates equals
  class-counting when every equivalence class has size two), which the
  commitment noted. The decisive convention was the one not enumerated.

## Lessons

Candidate for the sprint file: when the referent is a hand-computed
implementation of a published algorithm, cover the algorithm's likely
misreadings as candidate referents, not only its faithful conventions. A
definitional clause that can attach at two scopes (sum-level exclusion
versus parameter-level exclusion) is a predictable fork; compute both
values when cheap, and band at the referent's fidelity, not the
derivation's.
