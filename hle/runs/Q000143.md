## RIDM 27 Application

A long multi-select asking which listed infinite sets share the
cardinality of a real interval. Fifteen of the seventeen slots were
derivation-backed and matched the key. Two slots were contract
forks. One asked about strings over an alphabet "without any
limitation to length": I read it as finite strings of unbounded
length (countable) and the author reads it as admitting infinite
strings (continuum). The other asked about all points of a
countably-indexed real coordinate space: I derived the continuum,
and the key classifies it as strictly larger, which contradicts the
standard cardinal identity for a countable power of the continuum.
The committed string therefore differs from the key in two slots,
one a reading miss and one a key defect.

## Framework Observations

- The strings slot was decidable before reveal by the deletion test
  that the framework already names. Under the finite-string reading
  the qualifier does no work, since finite strings carry no length
  bound to remove; it does work only under the infinite-string
  reading. A qualifier that changes nothing under the committed
  reading is consumed only by the rival, and the commitment should
  have followed it. Instead the slot was resolved by a trap-design
  prior (the countability of finite strings is the classic
  counterintuitive fact), a meta-prior over the text's own signal.
- The coordinate-space slot is a checkable key defect: a countable
  product of continuum-sized sets has continuum cardinality. The
  result stays incorrect under the strict rule and the defective-key
  count stays frozen at two.
- Both single-slot variants were computed before reveal, so the
  post-reveal diagnosis is exact, but the keyed string was not in
  the candidate set because no reading of the text produces the
  defective slot.
- On a long multi-select, survival in the filtered pool is weak
  evidence about any single slot, since every reference model has
  many chances to slip; the per-statement filter rule was correctly
  judged non-decisive here.

## Lessons

- Run the deletion test on every qualifier before committing a
  reading: a qualifier that is redundant under the committed reading
  and load-bearing under the rival is typed evidence for the rival,
  and a design-intent prior cannot outrank it.
