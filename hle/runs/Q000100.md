## RIDM 20 Application

Three photo pairs of one species each, to be classified by which side shows
the invaded range. Two slots were called correctly, including the hardest:
the tropical tree was identified to species, its left photo geolocated to an
East Asian braided riverbed by flora and landform, and its right photo read
as native dry forest partly from wild-type fruit morphology; that
inference chain was the slot flagged as weakest and it held. The reversed
lupine slot also held.

The miss was the slot held with the most confidence: the crucifer forming a
dense monoculture carpet was classified as the invaded-range photo on the
strength of the canonical association between invasion and dense stands,
with the sparse mixed hedge read as native. The key has it reversed: the
carpet is the native-range photo and the sparse stand the invaded one.

## Framework Observations

- The failure is contrast transplantation applied to instances. The
  textbook claim behind the association is a population-level statistical
  contrast: the species is on average denser in its invaded range. A single
  photo from each range cannot measure a cross-range density contrast, and
  the species forms dense stands in parts of its native range too. Using a
  distributional contrast as a per-instance classifier is the same error as
  reusing a case-control direction on a within-population item, one level
  down: the recalled direction was sound, the licensed inference from one
  sample was not.
- The association was also the item's magnet: an ecology audience knows the
  dense-carpet-equals-invasion image, and the item pairs photos chosen to
  defeat exactly that heuristic. In a filtered pool, the vividness of the
  association was evidence against relying on it, per the standing
  magnet-pricing rule, and that rule was not consulted because the slot
  felt easy.
- Slot-level calibration inverted: the flagged weak slot survived and the
  confident slot failed. The compound band (low) was honest in aggregate,
  but the internal ordering shows confidence tracked familiarity of the
  cue rather than the validity of the inference licensed by it.
- The distinct-categories ensemble prior was correctly rejected (the key
  repeats one category three times); per-slot evidence over ensemble
  priors continues to hold.

## Lessons

Candidate for the sprint file: a recalled cross-population contrast (denser,
larger, earlier in the invaded range) licenses no per-instance
classification; one sample per side cannot measure a distributional
difference, and an available association strong enough to feel like a
classifier is exactly the association an adversarial item is built to
invert. Type the claim before using it: distribution-level recall supports
distribution-level conclusions only.
