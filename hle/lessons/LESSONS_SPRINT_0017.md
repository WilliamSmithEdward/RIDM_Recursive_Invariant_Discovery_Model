# Framework Lessons - Sprint 0017 (Q000161 to Q000170)

Distilled, content-free lessons from the sprint's runs in [../runs/](../runs/). Each lesson
names the run that produced it, the RIDM surface it touches, and the change it suggests. One
lessons file exists per sprint of ten runs.

Section numbers cited in this file and in this sprint's run logs refer to RIDM 30, the
specification version under evaluation during the sprint.

Consolidation status: L114 through L118 are consolidated into RIDM 31, released 2026-09-05.
The next sprint's lessons start in `lessons/LESSONS_SPRINT_0018.md`.

The sprint's dominant signal: six of ten runs scored, the high band five of five, and the
keyed answer entered the candidate set on all ten runs, the first full-coverage sprint since
the measure began. Every hit was derivation-led or instrument-led: a constraint solve, a
closed form anchored by numerical optimization, an instrumented chart, a stack identified
before it was computed on, a prescribed integral, and a construction whose one delicate step
was checked numerically. Every miss was a ranking-layer demotion of a keyed candidate already
in hand, and the four demotions share one mechanism: a rule applied outside the license its
item's class and register grant. An author-process prior overrode a fired representability
verdict on a casually authored item; an author-looseness model overrode a term-of-art
verdict on an expert item; a stem-consistency rule killed the keyed option on a generated
menu while the pool filter was under-weighted; and a technical reading of an everyday term,
backed by a manufactured designed-detail payoff, displaced the plain first-pass candidate on
a casual trivia item. The framework held the correct rule in each case and applied a
different one. One item was skipped without a run number when the harness safeguard blocked
replies to it.

## L114. A declared type is a given, and a process prior is a meta-prior (Q000161)

RIDM 30 surface: Sections 23 and 24, Control Laws 15 and 20. A stem declared the type of an
object it constructed from its givens, and under the committed reading most constructed
instances failed that type while under the rival every instance satisfied it. The
representability audit had fired: a reading whose instances fail the declared type is
refuted by that failure. It was overridden by an argument about how the author would have
coded the construction, which is an author-process prior, a meta-prior in the sense the
canonical-precedence rule already demotes to tie-breaking. Two folds: a declared type joins
the givens the representability audit protects, and a formula canonical in the setting
where it coincides with the declared type carries no canonicity into a setting where it does
not; and author-process priors are named among the meta-priors that never overturn.

## L115. The verdict ledger is a written list, and an expert's term binds (Q000163)

RIDM 30 surface: Sections 19 and 24, Control Laws 18 and 26. A term of art from the item's
field selected the keyed reading through the curricular-definition rule, and the selection
was overridden by a model of the author using the term loosely, on an expert-authored item
where that model is a competence downgrade. The override never had to name its evidence
because the ledger was reasoned about in prose rather than assembled. The rival's demotion
also rested on a domain-correctness objection that described the author's own predictable
construction, which the framework already types as evidence for that construction. Fold: the
ledger is written out as a list before commitment, with each override naming typed evidence
at matching scope, and the curricular-definition rule binds at the author's precision on
expert items.

## L116. On a generated menu only centrality and the filter are licensed (Q000164)

RIDM 30 surface: Sections 19 and 23, Control Laws 17 and 18. The rigor class was resolved as
generated and seed centrality was chosen as the instrument, correctly, and then a second
instrument ran unlicensed: options were killed for clauses contradicting the stem's own
framing. A generator's key is never checked against its own stem, so stem-option consistency
has no predictive force on that class. The pool filter was meanwhile applied at narrative
tier on the argument that an incoherent stem scatters the reference population, but the
menu's structure, a value repeated across three options plus one option echoing the stem's
framing, funnels every solver to the same option regardless of whether the calculation is
coherent; the attempt's own quick elimination landing there was the observation that the
population converges. Fold: on generated menus only seed centrality and the filter are
licensed, the population's convergence is predicted from the menu's funnel as much as from
the rigor class, and the option the solver's own elimination selects is the modal answer the
filter keys against.

## L117. A casual author's term carries everyday precision (Q000166)

RIDM 30 surface: Sections 19, 23, and 24, Control Laws 18, 20, and 22. A trivia-register
recall item used a size term that has a technical meaning in the field, and the technical
meaning was read as a class selector that changed the answer. The plain reading was the
first-pass candidate, and it was displaced by a rival whose advantages were the technical
reading and a period qualifier that bound tightly under it while discharging as
scene-setting under the plain reading. Both moves ran against provenance: canons index to
the provenance community, and in a casual register the term carries its everyday precision;
and load-bearingness discriminates between frames only on purposeful items, since on casual
items a period, scope, or size phrase discharges as scene-setting exactly as the
coherence-calibration rule says. Fold: term precision follows the author's register in both
directions, scene-setting discharge on a casual stem is not frame evidence, and the
first-pass plain candidate is displaced only by typed evidence.

## L118. Every rule in the verdict ledger carries a license (sprint synthesis)

RIDM 30 surface: Sections 19, 24, and 29, Control Laws 18 and 26. The four misses are one
failure seen four times: the framework licenses its menu instrument by rigor class and then
lets every other rule fire unconditionally. A coherence rule ran on a generated menu, a
technical-precision rule on a casual stem, a looseness model on an expert stem, and a
process prior over a representability verdict. The rigor class and the provenance register
license every rule, not only the instrument, and the verdict ledger records the license
beside the verdict: an entry whose license the item fails is void and struck rather than
weighed, and a licensed verdict is overridden only by typed evidence at matching scope. This
is the sharpening of the verdict-ledger discipline the previous three sprints introduced,
and it needs no new law: the class-licenses-the-instrument rule becomes the
class-licenses-every-rule rule at its existing home.
