# Framework Lessons - Sprint 0003 (Q000021 to Q000030)

Distilled, content-free lessons from the sprint's runs in [../runs/](../runs/). Each lesson
names the run that produced it, the RIDM surface it touches, and the change it suggests. One
lessons file exists per sprint of ten runs.

Section numbers cited in this file and in this sprint's run logs refer to RIDM 13, the
specification version under evaluation during the sprint.

Consolidation status: L25 through L32 are consolidated into RIDM 14, released 2026-08-30.
The next sprint's lessons start in `lessons/LESSONS_SPRINT_0004.md`.

The sprint's dominant signal: three high-band misses (Q000025, Q000027, Q000029) shared one
mechanism. Internal validation certified the attempt's own reasoning while the operative
success criterion was agreement with an authored answer key, and the items were retold,
casually authored, or generated. The decisive claim in a benchmark commitment is a
prediction of the key, and its support cannot exceed what is known about the key's author.

## L25. Coherence checks run over joint readings (Q000021)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 23 and 24. For transcription and perception tasks, enumerate joint
readings of the whole observation and let non-word or incoherent outcomes refute otherwise
plausible per-glyph readings. A task's guarantees about its own answer (wordhood, commonness,
a diacritic present) are discriminative evidence in the Section 23 sense. In Q000021 this
converted an ambiguous ligature into a forced call, confirmed at reveal.

## L26. Task-flagged observables are contract (Q000024, incorrect answer)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 8, 23, and 39. When the task explicitly points at an observable
("see the figure for the number of X"), that quantity is decisive by declaration and must be
observed before the commitment gate. In the same run, two related failures: the stem's own
labels and framing names were inherited as weak evidence when they are presentation in the
Control Law 25 sense, and the longest evidence block in the stem, a failed structural
search, was read as neutral detail when its argumentative function was refutation. Assign
every costly evidence block a direction of force before ranking answers; menu architecture
and mirror-pair patterns remain narrative-tier.

## L27. The key is an authored referent; predict it (Q000025 and Q000027, incorrect answers)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 10, 19, 21, and 29. In forced settings the operative criterion is
agreement with a key produced by an author. When a task retells a recognizable published
example, the key almost surely carries the source's published values, and retelling-induced
deviations are noise until proven load-bearing. When a task is informally authored, the key
follows the author's intended construction, and a stated constraint that stays slack under
every reading the attempt can construct signals that the author's reading is missing, not
that the constraint is decorative. Reconstructing the author's most available intended
construction is a required coverage probe; availability is legitimate evidence about the
author even though it is not evidence about ground truth.

## L28. Bands bind at the weakest decisive link (Q000025, Q000029, Q000030)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 21 and 29. A commitment's confidence band is the minimum over its
decisive links, explicitly including contract readings, key-referent inferences, and
convention choices, not the strength of the longest computational segment. A flawless
derivation conditioned on an uncertain branch is an inference at commitment time, and
internal cross-checks that exercise only the conditional branch do not support the branch
condition. The sprint's high-band record dropped from nine of nine to ten of thirteen on
exactly this failure.

## L29. Audit the givens for unused parameters (Q000026)

RIDM 13 surface: Section 23. A supplied constant or condition that plays no role in the
leading reading is evidence for a rival reading that consumes it; enumerating which givens
each candidate answer uses is a cheap coverage probe. The constructive twin of L26's
direction-of-force rule. In Q000026 an otherwise-unused cost parameter pointed to a hybrid
design that was the keyed answer. When an ambiguity does not change the committed answer
under any plausible resolution, record the dominance argument instead of resolving it.

## L30. Instrument measurement tasks (Q000028; Q000030 timestamps)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 39 and 40. When an image question asks for a number, measure
rather than eyeball: calibrate scale from many reference features, detect events
programmatically, and verify absence claims at the sensitivity of the smallest refuting
event. This produced an exact match in Q000028 and event locations accurate to fractions of
a second in Q000030.

## L31. Assess item rigor before setting the band (Q000029, incorrect answer)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 19, 21, and 48. Item rigor has observable tells: duplicated or
overlapping options, category-confused distractors, concept-dropping stems without a
well-formed question. On low-rigor items the key tracks the author's reasoning process, not
the field's consensus, characteristically sharing the correct derivation's first steps and
diverging by one flawed move. Cap such commitments at medium regardless of derivation
strength, and model the author's most probable chain, including its likely flawed step.

## L32. Resolve conventions before discretizing near a boundary (Q000030, incorrect answer)

RIDM 13 surface: Sections 11 and 30. A rounded or thresholded answer is a compound claim:
the measurement plus a convention. When the continuous value lands within measurement
uncertainty of the boundary, the two discretized answers are both live candidates and the
convention becomes the decisive claim; resolve it from domain practice (events are
timestamped at onset, not peak) instead of defaulting to the pipeline's internal choice.
Pricing the risk into the band is calibration, not resolution, when the residual is
resolvable at low cost.
